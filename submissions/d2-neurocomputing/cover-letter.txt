Dear Editors of Neurocomputing,

Please consider our manuscript, "When Do Rank-One Knowledge Edits Merge? A Gain-Screened Two-Regime Law of Edit Federation," for publication in Neurocomputing.

The paper addresses a fundamental question in neural network learning dynamics: when independently authored rank-one weight updates are combined into one network, under what conditions do they interfere destructively, and when do they reinforce constructively? We present the first systematic operating map of this edit-federation setting—22 model×layer cells across seven architecture families (1–20B parameters)—and discover a two-regime law governed by a measurable layer property.

The core contribution is a mechanism-level finding: merged edits' interference sign flips with the layer's perturbation gain. High-gain layers exhibit destructive over-amplification at every group size, geometry-predicted by key-vector alignment. Low-gain layers—deep layers of certain architectures—instead show constructive small-group reinforcement: aligned cross-talk raises the member edits' target logits before crossing to damage at an architecture-dependent threshold. To our knowledge, this sign reversal is without precedent in the model-merging literature, where alignment has uniformly been reported as harmful.

The regime structure holds from 1B to 20B across GPT, Llama, Qwen, Gemma, Phi, Mistral, and Pythia families. The gain screen orders regimes with Spearman ρ=0.79 and was preregistered: every threshold and directional prediction was frozen in writing before the corresponding experiments ran. We validate the mechanism causally by ablating the geometry component (AlphaEdit intervention at 8B scale) and observe the predicted damage removal. The paper reports not only confirmed predictions but also the intermediate interpretations our own follow-up tests refuted, in service of reproducible mechanism discovery.

For the neural-network learning-systems community, this refines the interference picture inherited from task-vector merging—where sign conflict and magnitude collision are the canonical culprits and aligned overlap is engineered away—by showing that, for rank-one knowledge edits at the readout, aligned overlap's effect carries a sign that depends on a measurable layer property. For maintainers of knowledge-edited models, the map converts into a retrospectively evaluated admission framework: gain-screen the target layer, then geometry-order candidates.

The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere. A companion mechanism-level study of single-edit collateral damage is under review at IEEE TETCI and is cited where relevant; the present paper's federation setting, two-regime discovery, and preregistered map do not overlap with it. Code, edit vectors, and result artifacts are openly available at Zenodo (DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.21405273) and GitHub (https://github.com/PeterPonyu/edit-federation-map).
