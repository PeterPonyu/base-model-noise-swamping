\begin{abstract}
Methods for combining independently computed weight updates---task arithmetic, adapter
fusion, editing composition---share one assumption: overlap between updates is
monotonically harmful, so alignment should be minimized or whitened away. We falsify it
for rank-one knowledge edits. Across \nCells{} model--layer cells
spanning \nFamilies{} architecture families from 1B to 20B parameters, aligned cross-talk
between merged edits \emph{reverses sign}. In some layers merged edits damage one another
as predicted; in others---deep layers of several architectures, at every scale
tested---the same interference systematically \emph{raises} the member edits' target
logits before crossing to damage at an architecture-dependent group size. This is
neither sign cancellation nor a failure of
geometry to track cross-talk; what flips is that cross-talk's effect at the readout. One
measurable scalar, the layer's perturbation \emph{gain}, orders the regimes (rank
correlation \ordSpearman{} with constructive fraction, $p=\ordSpearmanP{}$, \nCells{}
cells; gate constants and the directional prediction frozen before the runs), and a
first-order readout model grounds it: gain ranks cells by the magnitude of the
readout slope at the edit's operating point (a rank-level proxy, not a point
estimate); the regime is that slope's sign. Two boundaries scope
the law: key geometry predicts damage only in small merge groups, and in most low-gain
cells edits also install weakly---so the constructive regime largely marks where edits
should \emph{not} be placed, which the screen identifies a priori. Within these
boundaries the map yields a gain-screened, geometry-ordered admission framework:
retrospectively it avoids \benefitHighQtwofive{} logits of damage per admitted edit
versus random admission at a 25\% budget in high-gain regimes.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
Composing independently computed weight-space updates into a single network has become a
standard operation on trained models: task vectors are added, adapters are fused, and
low-rank corrections are stacked, all without retraining. The operation is a
superposition of perturbations in one shared parameter matrix, and its central open
question is a question about neural computation rather than about bookkeeping: when
several perturbations are applied at once, how does the network's readout combine them,
and can the combined effect be anticipated from the perturbations themselves?

Rank-one knowledge editing gives that question an unusually clean instance. Deployed
models carry factual errors and stale or hallucinated associations that surface only
after release~\cite{hallucinationsurvey2023}, and correcting them one fact at a time is
far cheaper than retraining. Operators therefore increasingly apply \emph{post-hoc
knowledge edits}: rank-one or low-rank weight updates that install, correct, or delete an
individual fact in place~\cite{rome2022,memit2023,alphaedit2024}. Each such edit is a
single rank-one perturbation of one linear map, specified by an explicit key/value pair,
so the perturbation that one edit delivers at another edit's key has a closed form in the
stored vectors---no training probes, no fitted surrogate. As the practice matures, the
unit of maintenance also shifts from a single edit to a fleet of them: several teams
author updates independently---new product facts, corrected entities, retracted
claims---and whoever owns the model must decide which to combine into the next build. We
call this \emph{edit federation}: merging many independently authored edits into one
deployed model. It inherits, in analytically tractable form, the question the
model-merging literature has studied only for task vectors and fine-tuned adapters: when
do independently computed updates interfere, and can the interference be anticipated
before any merge is committed?

The prevailing answer is monotone: overlap causes interference. Task-arithmetic merging
attributes damage to sign conflict and magnitude collision~\cite{ties2023,dare2023};
editing-composition methods enforce orthogonality between successive edits precisely
because overlap is assumed harmful~\cite{memitmerge2025,oedit2024,orthoedit2026a,orthoedit2026b};
SVD-based merging takes the same stance --- Task Singular Vectors~\cite{tsv2024}
defines an \emph{unsigned} singular-vector-overlap interference measure and whitens
the overlap away, treating alignment as exclusively harmful; and
predictors built from update geometry report stable, non-inverting
signs~\cite{demystify2026}. The nearest prior result adds one nonmonotonicity---near
parallel adapter updates over-amplify and hurt~\cite{mergeprobe2026}---but its direction is
uniformly ``similarity hurts,'' measured on adapters with training probes, with no scale or
architecture axis.

We show the picture for rank-one knowledge edits is qualitatively richer, and we turn it
into an operational rule a maintainer can apply. Our contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{An operating map of edit federation} (\nCells{} model$\times$layer cells,
\nFamilies{} architecture families, 1--20B, group sizes 2--20 with saturation confirmed
through 100, three seeds, pre-registered gates): where key-geometry coherence predicts merge interference, at which group sizes the
signal is testable, and where damage saturates. To our knowledge this includes the first
edit-federation measurements at and beyond 12B scale (to 20B), where we also find
testable interference thinning out (Section~\ref{sec:limits}).
\item \textbf{A two-regime law.} High-gain regimes are destructive at every group size with
geometry-predicted damage (over-amplification, consistent with the adapter result
of~\cite{mergeprobe2026}). Low-gain regimes are \emph{constructive at small group sizes}:
aligned cross-talk raises the member edits' target logits, with an architecture-dependent
crossover to damage. To our knowledge the \emph{sign} of this regime is without precedent:
prior nonmonotone merging interactions~\cite{forkmerge2023,mergeprobe2026} all report that
alignment hurts; we report a regime where it systematically helps.
\item \textbf{A measurable screen.} A single scalar per model$\times$layer---the
perturbation \emph{gain}, measured once per model version from a small calibration set
of merges on a staging copy (the per-edit ordering step below is vector-only; the layer
screen is not)---orders
the regimes (Spearman \ordSpearman{} with constructive fraction across all cells; the
directional form of this claim, $\rho\leq-0.7$, was frozen in writing before the runs,
while its measured value is descriptive --- Section~\ref{sec:prelim} draws the line
exactly). The
regime is depth-gated \emph{within} a model at fixed scale, is not scale-gated (a 1.5B model
supplies the most extreme constructive trajectory --- though that model's pre-modern
architecture is itself a residual axis, as Section~\ref{sec:law} notes), and is not
family-gated. We state explicitly
what the screen does not explain: at matched gain, architecture residuals in
constructiveness remain.
\item \textbf{Mechanistic localization, and a first-order readout model.} The flip is not
cosine sign-cancellation (signed
and absolute interference indices are numerically identical everywhere; negative-cosine key
mass is negligible in all cells) and not differential geometry-tracking (the received
cross-talk norm is geometry-predicted at $\rho\approx0.99$ in every cell we re-analyzed).
Aligned
cross-talk arrives everywhere; what flips is its effect at the readout: a boost in
low-gain regimes, a penalty in high-gain regimes. Section~\ref{sec:mech} gives this
formal structure. A short proposition, proved from a first-order expansion under stated
assumptions, identifies the two quantities we measure---cell gain and constructive
fraction---as estimates of the magnitude and the sign of the readout slope at the edit's
operating point; a separately labeled conjecture supplies the operating-point account
that ties that sign to the layer's gain, with three predictions, one already confirmed
and one decisive falsification test we name but do not run.
\item \textbf{An operational implication for knowledge maintenance.} Gain-screen the target layer, then geometry-order
candidate edits. We evaluate it retrospectively across the map; the same stored key/value
quantities also support a held-out per-edit damage predictor (gate $\rho=\predictorRho{}$
at the reference layer).
\end{enumerate}

The gate constants, the directional ordering claim, and the per-cell directional
predictions were frozen in writing before the corresponding runs; Section~\ref{sec:prelim}
states precisely which quantities that covers and which remain descriptive, and
Section~\ref{sec:falsified} reports the intermediate interpretations that our own
follow-up tests refuted.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
\textbf{Model merging and task arithmetic.} Combining independently trained models ranges
from plain weight averaging~\cite{modelsoups2022} to curvature- and alignment-aware fusion:
Fisher-weighted averaging~\cite{fishermerging2022}, permutation alignment before
averaging~\cite{gitrebasin2023}, and closed-form dataless fusion~\cite{regmean2023}. Task
arithmetic recast fine-tuning deltas as composable \emph{task
vectors}~\cite{taskarithmetic2023}, after which interference-aware methods treated the
damage as sign disagreement plus magnitude collision~\cite{ties2023,dare2023}, with per-layer
refinements from singular-vector analysis~\cite{tsv2024} and learned per-layer merge
coefficients~\cite{adamerging2024}; a recent survey maps the broader
landscape~\cite{mergingsurvey2024}. On predicting interference from update geometry,
interpretable linear predictors have been fit with stable, non-inverting
signs~\cite{demystify2026}, and mergeability has been attributed to base-model knowledge
rather than geometry~\cite{willitmerge2026}. The nearest neighbor predicts set-level
interference from update alignment and reports an over-amplification nonmonotonicity, in
which near-parallel adapter updates roughly double and hurt~\cite{mergeprobe2026}. None of
these carries a scale or architecture-family axis, and none reports a constructive regime.
Our units differ: they are train-free rank-one edits whose key/value structure gives
interference a closed per-edit form (Section~\ref{sec:prelim}), and our map spans
\nFamilies{} families to 20B.

\textbf{Knowledge editing.} Methods to install or correct a fact in place fall into
families: locate-then-edit approaches solve for a rank-one or low-rank weight change at a
causally implicated layer~\cite{rome2022,memit2023,pmet2024,alphaedit2024}; hypernetwork
editors learn to predict the change from the edit request~\cite{decao2021,mend2022}; and
memory- or adaptor-based editors keep the base weights frozen and hold edits in an external
module~\cite{serac2022,grace2023,wise2024}. The locate-then-edit family builds on evidence
that factual associations are localized to identifiable
components~\cite{knowledgeneurons2022,rome2022}, and surveys and benchmarks catalogue the
fast-growing method and evaluation landscape~\cite{kesurvey2023,knowedit2024}. We federate
edits from the locate-then-edit family, whose closed-form key/value structure is what makes
merge cross-talk analytically tractable.

\textbf{Knowledge-editing composition.} Batch and sequential editing assume overlap is
harmful and engineer it away: conflict resolution over identical keys~\cite{memitmerge2025}
and orthogonality constraints between successive edits~\cite{oedit2024,orthoedit2026a,%
orthoedit2026b}, extended to multilingual sequential editing by projecting each
language's updates onto the orthogonal complement of prior updates~\cite{langedit2025};
a null-space reproduction reports positive co-directional damage
accumulation~\cite{alphaeditrepro2026}. Two works deserve direct comparison.
MEMIT-Merge~\cite{memitmerge2025} is the closest key-space precedent: it shows that
\emph{identical} keys in same-subject batches collide and resolves the collision inside
the editor's solve --- our map instead concerns \emph{distinct} edits with graded key
overlap, where MEMIT-Merge's premise (overlap $\Rightarrow$ damage) holds in high-gain
layers and reverses in low-gain ones. Closest in units is the multilingual
edit-merging study of~\cite{mke_merge2026}, which empirically compares merging
strategies for independently computed language-specific edit batches; it treats
interference purely as loss to minimize and does not map its sign, depth dependence,
or predictability --- exactly the questions our operating map answers. We measure,
rather than assume, the sign of overlap's effect, and find regimes where it reverses.

\textbf{Side effects of editing.} A parallel line documents that edits damage unrelated
knowledge: single edits induce knowledge conflict and distortion~\cite{pitfalls2024}, a
handful of sequential edits can trigger model collapse~\cite{butterfly2024}, and editing at
scale drives gradual and then catastrophic forgetting~\cite{seqforget2024}. This collateral
damage is exactly what a federation maintainer must anticipate before merging; the per-edit
damage predictor we connect to the admission rule (Section~\ref{sec:rule}) is a screen
against it.

\textbf{Interference under aligned updates.} In multi-task learning, negative transfer can
be \emph{worse} under highly consistent gradients~\cite{forkmerge2023}, the cleanest
precedent that alignment is not protective; gradient-surgery methods respond to the same
tension~\cite{pcgrad2020,cagrad2021}. Continual learning attacks the same parameter-space
interference from the other side, by protecting weights an old task deemed
important~\cite{ewc2017} or projecting updates to respect stored past-task
gradients~\cite{gem2017}. These literatures never index the effect's sign by architecture,
depth, or a measurable layer property.

\textbf{Architecture-level perturbation transmission.} Our measurements show that how
strongly a layer transmits value-space perturbations to the output distribution varies
by orders of magnitude across architectures and depths (the gain of
Section~\ref{sec:law}); the gain screen gives this an operational form and an
interference consequence, and our results across seven families show the low-gain
regime is not specific to one family.

\section{Preliminaries: Rank-One Edits and Exact Merge Cross-Talk}\label{sec:prelim}
A ROME-style edit installs $\Delta W_a = r_a k_a^{\top}/(k_a^{\top}k_a)$. Federating a
group $G$ deploys $W + \sum_{b\in G}\Delta W_b$; the value delivered at edit $a$'s key
is $r_a + d_a$ with exact cross-talk
\begin{equation}
d_a \;=\; \sum_{b\neq a} r_b\,\frac{k_b^{\top}k_a}{k_b^{\top}k_b},
\label{eq:crosstalk}
\end{equation}
closed-form in the stored vectors. Outcome per observation:
$\mathrm{drop}=\mathrm{logit}_{\mathrm{solo}}-\mathrm{logit}_{\mathrm{merged}}$ at the
target token ($\mathrm{drop}<0$: merging \emph{helped}). Here $k_a$ is the edit's key (the subject representation at the edited layer) and $r_a$ its
value residual; we use a native ROME implementation with fp32 value optimization, $n=200$
edits per cell, and three seeds drawing independently sampled edit sets (seeded
shuffles of the full 21{,}919-record bank; any two seeds' 200-edit banks share 2--5
records). Our ROME uses the identity-covariance (unwhitened) rank-one solve;
canonical ROME's covariance-weighted variant replaces $k_a$ with $C^{-1}k_a$ in
the left factor. Eq.~\ref{eq:crosstalk} and the dose are defined for the
unwhitened update used throughout.
The \emph{dose} of observation
$a$ is $(d_a\!\cdot\!r_a)/\lVert r_a\rVert^2$, the received cross-talk relative to the
edit's own delivery, and a cell's \emph{gain} is the median of $|\mathrm{drop}|/\text{dose}$
over positive-dose observations (pooled over $g\leq20$): the logit response per unit
relative dose, measuring how strongly the layer transmits value-space perturbations to the
readout.

\textbf{Interference indices and gate.} We summarize the interference an edit receives with
two indices,
\begin{equation}
I_{\cos}(a)=\lVert k_a\rVert\sum_{b\neq a} S_b\,\bigl|\cos(k_b,k_a)\bigr|,
\qquad
I_{\mathrm{mag}}(a)=\lVert k_a\rVert\sum_{b\neq a} S_b,
\label{eq:indices}
\end{equation}
the first geometry-weighted and the second a magnitude-only scaffold, where $S_b$ scales
each donor by its value norm. The geometry discriminant is the partial Spearman correlation
$\rho(I_{\cos},\mathrm{drop}\mid I_{\mathrm{mag}})$, further guarded by an own-magnitude
partial. A pre-registered gate, frozen before any multi-model run, declares a cell's
geometry signal real if and only if this partial exceeds $+0.15$ at two or more group
sizes in two or more seeds, where a group size counts only if interference is
non-negligible, unsaturated (argmax-loss below $0.8$), and coherent (raw
$\rho(I_{\cos},\mathrm{drop})\geq 0.30$). The quorum rule spans many implicit
comparisons, so we calibrated it against a within-cell permutation null at the
reference cell and then at every other cell of the map: over $2{,}000$ replicates
per cell that shuffle drops against the geometry pairs inside every (group size,
seed) sub-cell, the full rule never fires in any of the 22 cells (false-positive
rate $0/2{,}000$ everywhere), and every qualifying sub-cell's observed partial sits
at the permutation floor (one-sided $p<1/2{,}000$, the resolution of the test).

\textbf{Reproducibility parameters.} Edits use a native ROME implementation: a
rank-one update to the MLP down-projection at the stated layer, with the value vector
optimized in fp32 for 20 Adam steps at learning rate $0.1$ and a weight-decay anchor
of $10^{-3}$ toward the pre-edit value. All \nCells{} map cells --- including the 12B,
14B, and 20B models, run on large-memory cards --- load model weights in fp32; the only
reduced-precision run in the paper is the out-of-map 13B replication cell of
Section~\ref{sec:law}, loaded in bf16 and flagged there. Layers are selected by relative
depth: 75\% of the layer stack is the primary cell per model, with 50\% (and two
deeper, 86--88\%) contrast cells as annotated in Table~\ref{tab:width}. Edit banks are drawn from
CounterFact by seeded shuffle as described above, with no further filtering; the
known-fact and successful-edit filters apply only at measurement, where probes are
restricted to facts the base model answers correctly and observations to edits that
installed. Gain is calibrated from the same $200$-edit merge sets pooled over
$g\leq20$ (positive-dose observations), about 91\,s of GPU time per cell in our
setting. The full frozen gate constants are: geometry partial threshold $+0.15$;
coherence $\rho(I_{\cos},\mathrm{drop})\geq0.30$; negligibility, median
$|\mathrm{drop}|\geq0.1$ logits or argmax-loss rate $\geq0.05$; saturation ceiling,
argmax-loss $>0.8$. AI-assisted tools (Claude, Anthropic) were used in the research
process to support code authoring, experiment orchestration, and data analysis,
including generation of figure-plotting code from experimental result files; all code,
experimental designs, and analytical outputs were reviewed and verified by the author.

\textbf{Pre-registration ledger, and what is \emph{not} pre-registered.} Because
``pre-registered'' is used loosely in this literature, we state the boundary exactly.
Three things were frozen in dated, written documents that preceded the corresponding
runs. (i) The gate rule and all its constants --- the $+0.15$ partial threshold, the
$0.30$ coherence rule, the negligibility and saturation ceilings, and the own-magnitude
guard --- frozen before any multi-model run. (ii) The ordering claim, frozen as a
\emph{directional} prediction with a numeric bound: Spearman(gain, constructive
fraction) $\leq-0.7$, restated verbatim at each addendum as the cell count grew.
(iii) Per-cell directional predictions (regime, and in most cases a constructive-fraction
band) for the expansion cells, including the depth-contrast and pre-modern GPT-2-XL
cells, written before those cells ran.

Three things are \emph{not} pre-registered and we do not present them as such. The
measured value of the ordering statistic --- \ordSpearman{} and its confidence interval
--- is a descriptive estimate; what was frozen is the direction and the $\leq-0.7$ bound
that it satisfies, and the analysis artifact carrying this table is labelled descriptive
and non-pre-registered accordingly. The three final-wave cells (Mistral-Nemo-12B,
gemma-2-9b, GPT-NeoX-20B) were added after the last prediction freeze with no per-cell
directional predictions; we therefore report the ordering both with and without them
($-0.811$ over 19 cells, \ordSpearman{} over \nCells{}). The out-of-map 13B replication
cell of Section~\ref{sec:law} was likewise run after the freeze, without a per-cell
prediction, and is excluded from the headline statistic. The gain$=8$ banding used in
the figures is a display choice, not a frozen threshold (Section~\ref{sec:law}).
The ledger --- the frozen documents with their dates, the complete
prediction$\to$outcome record scored cell-by-cell (12 scored predictions: 9 held, 1 held
with a noted deviation, 1 partial, 1 near-miss, with 3 cells declared exploratory at
freeze time), and the post-freeze additions --- is included in the research-data deposit
and available to reviewers.

\section{The Operating Map under the Pre-Registered Gate}\label{sec:map}
At the reference cell (Llama-3.2-1B, layer~12, 75\% relative depth) the gate passes:
geometry predicts merge damage at $g{=}2$ (per-seed partials \refPartialsGtwo{}), $g{=}3$
(\refPartialsGthree{}), and $g{=}5$ (\refPartialsGfive{}); $g{=}10$ fails the coherence
rule (a transition band) and $g\geq20$ saturates; extended runs at the reference cell and
at the deep Qwen-1.5B cell confirm full saturation at $g{=}50$ and $g{=}100$ (argmax-loss
rates $0.89$--$1.00$ across seeds), which is why all pooled analyses use $g\leq20$. Two
distinct boundaries follow, and they scope the result differently. The geometry result is
established only for $g\leq5$: that is the range over which the partial correlation
qualifies under the frozen gate. At $g{=}10$ damage remains gradated --- merges still
produce measurable, non-saturated per-edit damage --- but the geometry signal fails the
coherence rule, so the predictive claim does not extend there.

Table~\ref{tab:width} reports the frozen gate's verdict for every cell in the map, with
each cell's boundary defined as the largest qualifying group size in $\{2,3,5,10,20\}$
under the frozen prereg, together with each cell's peak geometry partial and its
across-seed range. In total the ROME map comprises \nSubcells{} (group size, seed)
sub-cells and \nObsRome{} individual merge observations; the editor- and
dataset-generality waves of Section~\ref{sec:editors} add 135 sub-cells and
\nObsEditors{} more, for \nObsTotal{} measured observations across the study. These
counts are merge observations (one edit inside one merged group); each aggregates the
target-logit change over the base-known probe set, so probe-level filters affect what
is averaged within an observation, not the observation counts themselves.
Figure~\ref{fig:gateev} plots the evidence behind every verdict in the table ---
per-cell geometry partials by group size with across-seed ranges against the frozen
$+0.15$ gate --- and Figure~\ref{fig:dose} shows the raw damage dose--response those
verdicts rest on: median per-edit damage grows monotonically in group size in every
cell of the map, spanning almost four orders of magnitude across cells at matched $g$.

\subsection{A first-order readout model of the sign reversal}\label{sec:mech}
Section~\ref{sec:law} establishes \emph{that} the effect of aligned cross-talk reverses
sign; this subsection states what has to be true of the readout for that to happen, and
separates the part that follows from a derivation from the part that remains an empirical
conjecture with a named falsification test.

Fix an observation: edit $a$, merged in a group $G$, receiving cross-talk $d_a$ from
Eq.~\ref{eq:crosstalk}. Decompose the received cross-talk into a component along the
edit's own delivery direction and a residual,
\begin{equation}
d_a=\delta_a\,r_a+w_a,\qquad \langle w_a,r_a\rangle=0,\qquad
\delta_a=\frac{\langle d_a,r_a\rangle}{\lVert r_a\rVert^{2}},
\label{eq:decomp}
\end{equation}
so that $\delta_a$ is exactly the \emph{dose} of Section~\ref{sec:prelim}. Let
$\ell_a(t)$ denote the target-token logit obtained when the value delivered at $a$'s key
is $t\,r_a$, all else held fixed; the solo edit is $\ell_a(1)$ and the merged
observation is $\ell_a(1+\delta_a)$ up to the residual $w_a$.

\begin{assumption}[Scalar readout along the delivery axis]\label{as:scalar}
The target logit depends on the received cross-talk only through $\delta_a$ to first
order: the contribution of the orthogonal residual $w_a$ is $o(|\delta_a|)$.
\end{assumption}
\begin{assumption}[Local smoothness]\label{as:smooth}
$\ell_a$ is continuously differentiable on a neighbourhood of $t=1$ containing
$1+\delta_a$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}[Gain and constructive fraction are the magnitude and the sign of one
slope]\label{prop:slope}
Under Assumptions~\ref{as:scalar}--\ref{as:smooth},
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{drop}_a\;=\;\ell_a(1)-\ell_a(1+\delta_a)\;=\;-\,\ell_a'(1)\,\delta_a\;+\;o(|\delta_a|).
\label{eq:firstorder}
\end{equation}
Consequently, for positive-dose observations with $\ell_a'(1)\neq0$, in the limit
$\delta_a\to0$ we have $\operatorname{sign}(\mathrm{drop}_a)=
-\operatorname{sign}(\ell_a'(1))$ and $|\mathrm{drop}_a|/\delta_a\to|\ell_a'(1)|$.
The sign statement is uninformative for cells whose slope is near zero, which is
where the boundary cells of Section~\ref{sec:law} sit; we therefore report those
cells separately and do not rest the regime assignment on them. A
cell's \emph{gain}, the median of $|\mathrm{drop}|/\mathrm{dose}$ over positive-dose
observations (pooled over $g\leq20$; see the discussion following for the
resulting finite-dose bias), is therefore a rank-level proxy for the median
$|\ell_a'(1)|$ in that cell rather than a point estimate of it, and its
\emph{constructive fraction} a proxy for $\Pr[\ell_a'(1)>0]$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Assumption~\ref{as:scalar} lets the merged logit be written $\ell_a(1+\delta_a)+
o(|\delta_a|)$; Assumption~\ref{as:smooth} gives the first-order expansion
$\ell_a(1+\delta_a)=\ell_a(1)+\ell_a'(1)\delta_a+o(|\delta_a|)$. Subtracting yields
Eq.~\ref{eq:firstorder}; dividing by $\delta_a>0$ and letting $\delta_a\to0$ gives the
two limit statements.
\end{proof}

The proposition is not a result about which layers are destructive --- it carries no
empirical content on its own. What it does is tie the two statistics this paper measures
to a single object. Gain and constructive fraction are not two independent measurements
that happen to correlate; under Assumptions~\ref{as:scalar}--\ref{as:smooth} they are the
magnitude and the sign of the same derivative, which is why an ordering between them is
a statement about one readout curve rather than a coincidence. It also explains a
detail of Section~\ref{sec:law} that would otherwise look accidental: the
sign-carrying statistic is $\rho(\mathrm{dose},\mathrm{drop})$, not any norm-based
index, because Eq.~\ref{eq:firstorder} makes dose the signed regressor and $-\ell_a'(1)$
its coefficient.

The identification is asymptotic in $\delta_a$, and our estimator pools over a dose range
that is not small in that sense: median dose grows by a factor of roughly 21--26 from
$g{=}2$ to $g{=}20$ (0.046 to 0.994 at the reference cell; 0.017 to 0.433 at the deep 14B
cell), and the recovered ratio drifts correspondingly --- per-group-size gain falls
$34.4\to17.3$ at the reference cell and $4.98\to1.43$ at the 14B cell, a factor of 2.0
and 3.5 respectively. The pooled gain is therefore best read as a rank-level proxy for
$|\ell_a'|$ rather than a point estimate of it: it is stable enough in \emph{ordering}
that every robustness variant we ran preserves the cross-cell ranking, including
recomputing the outcome at fixed $g{=}2$ with no pooling at all
($\rho=-0.81$; Section~\ref{sec:law}), which is the form of the statistic the
small-dose limit actually licenses.

The empirical content sits in the next statement, which we do not prove.

\begin{conjecture}[Operating-point account of the two regimes]\label{conj:opt}
Each cell has a value $t^{*}_a$ at which $\ell_a$ peaks. Editing to a fixed optimization
budget places the solo operating point $t{=}1$ \emph{past} $t^{*}_a$ in layers that
transmit value perturbations strongly and \emph{below} $t^{*}_a$ in layers that transmit
them weakly. Then $\ell_a'(1)<0$ (destructive) in the first case and $\ell_a'(1)>0$
(constructive) in the second; and because $|\ell_a'|$ is small on the shallow ascending
branch and large on the steep descending branch, the magnitude of the slope orders its
sign across cells --- the ordering Section~\ref{sec:law} measures. Crossover in group
size occurs where the accumulated dose first carries $1+\delta_a$ past $t^{*}_a$.
\end{conjecture}

The conjecture makes three predictions with different current status.
\emph{(P1) Reducing dose at a fixed layer extends the constructive window in $g$.}
Confirmed: at the deep 14B cell, AlphaEdit suppresses cross-talk magnitude about
five-fold and the cell remains constructive past $g{=}20$, where under ROME the geometry
partials had already reverted positive (Section~\ref{sec:editors}).
\emph{(P2) Within a cell, the sign of drop must flip when the sign of dose flips.}
This is the decisive test and we do not run it: aligned cross-talk is near-universal in
our cells (Section~\ref{sec:law}), so negative-dose observations are far too rare to
support it --- testing it requires donor sets constructed to be anti-aligned to the
receiving edit's key, which no natural edit bank supplies.
\emph{(P3) Crossover occurs at a cell-constant accumulated dose, independent of how that
dose is assembled} (many weak donors or few strong ones).
The conjecture is falsified if reducing per-donor dose at a fixed layer moves the
crossover to \emph{smaller} $g$, if a cell's drop sign fails to flip under sign-reversed
dose while remaining first-order in $|\delta_a|$, or if two cells at matched gain land on
opposite sides of the sign boundary beyond sampling error. The last of these is a live
test that currently passes: no cell in the map lands significantly on the wrong side of
its regime (Section~\ref{sec:law}), although constructiveness \emph{magnitude} at
matched gain does vary by architecture, which is why we state the conjecture as a
rank-level account rather than a sufficient one. Assumption~\ref{as:scalar} is the
model's main exposure: it discards the orthogonal component $w_a$, and a cell in which
$w_a$ carried a systematic logit effect would break the identification in
Proposition~\ref{prop:slope} without touching any measurement reported here.

\textbf{Threshold, gradedness, and robustness of the ordering.} The
gain--constructiveness relation is graded rather than dichotomous, and we state the
split explicitly: the ``high-gain''/``low-gain'' banding in the figures cuts at gain
$8$, a display choice --- within the \nCells{}-cell map the interval between the
boundary cells (deep Qwen-3B, gain $3.6$, and Mistral-Nemo-12B, gain $10.7$) contains no
cell, so any cut inside it produces the same grouping --- while the
frozen pre-registration made \emph{per-cell directional} predictions, not a numeric
threshold. The out-of-map 13B replication cell discussed below falls inside that
interval (gain \gainThirteenB{}), so for that cell the banding is genuinely
cut-dependent; this is one reason we treat it as a boundary cell and report it
separately rather than folding it into the banded figures. Its constructive fraction
(\fracThirteenB{}) sits with the other boundary cells (Mistral-Nemo $0.423$, deep
Qwen-3B $0.430$) rather than with either band. (This graded relation is distinct from the refuted single dose-continuum
of Section~\ref{sec:falsified}, which concerned collapse at matched dimensionless
dose.) Five cells cross the pooled constructive-fraction midpoint, all in the
low-gain band and none in the high-gain band, and in each of the five the median
signed drop at $g{=}2$ is significantly \emph{negative}: bootstrap 95\% CIs lie
entirely below zero over 600 pooled observations per cell, sign-test
$p\leq 8.7\times10^{-6}$ --- including the smallest-magnitude cell, GPT-NeoX-20B
(median $-0.0009$, CI $[-0.0012,-0.0006]$), whose damage the frozen gate correctly
classes negligible and untestable for geometry: we cite it here only as evidence that
the sign pattern is systematic, never as an operationally meaningful boost. The
constructive regime is therefore real in sign everywhere it appears, while its
magnitude at the lowest-gain cells is practically negligible --- the admission value
concentrates in high-gain regimes (Section~\ref{sec:rule}). The headline ordering is
robust on two further axes. First, it is not a re-description of shared damage
magnitudes: correlating gain against the magnitude-decoupled effect statistic
$\rho(\mathrm{dose},\mathrm{drop})$ gives Spearman $\raControl{}$
($p=\raControlP{}$); estimating gain on a random half of each cell's observations
and the constructive fraction on the disjoint other half leaves the ordering intact
($\rho=-0.81$, $p=4.4\times10^{-6}$), as does computing the outcome at fixed $g{=}2$
with no pooling ($\rho=-0.81$; both quantities at $g{=}2$: $\rho=-0.76$,
$p=4.5\times10^{-5}$). Second, it does not depend on treating layers of one model as
independent: leave-one-family-out Spearman remains within \lofoRange{} for every one
of the seven families, a family-cluster bootstrap gives 95\% CI \famBootCI{}, and
collapsing the map to seven family medians still yields $\rho=\famLevelRho{}$
($p=\famLevelP{}$) --- the cluster-safe statistic we regard as the primary
inferential result, with the 22-cell $p$ read descriptively.

\textbf{Gain also governs whether edits install at all.} The same scalar that orders
merge interference orders solo-edit success: Spearman(gain, esr) $=+0.83$
($p=1.4\times10^{-6}$) across the 22 cells (Table~\ref{tab:gain}, last column).
Within the map, every high-gain cell installs edits at esr $\geq0.91$ and every low-gain
cell sits at $0.84$ or below, collapsing to $0.01$--$0.05$ at the lowest-gain cells (the
held-out 13B cell later in this section is the one measured exception, and we treat it as
such). This is one
phenomenon, not two: a low-gain layer transmits value-space perturbations weakly to
the readout, so an edit's own correction rarely clears the argmax margin (low esr),
neighbors' cross-talk is weak (small drops), and what coupling remains is
constructive. Two consequences must be stated plainly. First, the constructive-regime
measurements at the extreme cells are logit-level cross-talk between installed weight
perturbations, most of which do not individually flip the argmax --- the sign
statistics of the previous paragraph are physics of the perturbations, not
interactions between successfully deployed edits. Second, for a maintainer the deep
low-gain layers are doubly poor edit sites: edits barely install there, and the
admission machinery of Section~\ref{sec:rule} correctly concentrates its value in
high-gain regimes. Our per-model layer choices follow the fixed relative-depth
convention of Section~\ref{sec:prelim}; for the pre-modern architectures in
particular, causal-tracing evidence~\cite{rome2022} places the editable band well
below 75\% relative depth, which is consistent with the esr collapse we observe
there.

window-shifts-up shape that Section~\ref{sec:map} reports for the other boundary cells.

Two things about this cell are worth stating plainly. First, it is a genuine held-out
test of the screen's ordering, not a re-description: the cell was run after the ordering
statistic was fixed, its gain places it between the two banding anchors, and its
constructive fraction (\fracThirteenB{}) and $g{=}2$ dose correlation
(\rhoGtwoThirteenB{}) both land on the side the screen predicts. Second, it separates two
things the rest of the map confounds. Its edits install at esr \esrThirteenB{}, far above
the $0.84$ ceiling that holds for every low-gain cell in the map, while its small-$g$
cross-talk is nonetheless constructive. Proposition~\ref{prop:slope} makes this
unsurprising: constructiveness is a statement about the \emph{sign of the readout slope}
at the operating point, whereas install success is a statement about the target logit
clearing its competitors --- different thresholds, which the extreme low-gain cells happen
to cross together and this one does not. The practical reading is that the ``do not place
edits here'' advice of Section~\ref{sec:law} is a property of the extreme low-gain cells,
not an entailment of constructiveness itself. Three caveats keep this to its size: it is
one cell with a Mixed verdict, its model was loaded in bf16 rather than the fp32 used
throughout the map (with the same weight-reconstruction fidelity check applied,
worst-seed relative error $0.9\%$), and no per-cell directional prediction was frozen
for it.

\textbf{What the screen does not explain.} At matched gain ($\approx3.4$), GPT-2-XL is far
more constructive than the deep Qwen layers (0.711 versus 0.35--0.43), and crossover-$g$ is
not a clean function of gain (Qwen-1.5B L24, gain 1.1, crosses near $g{=}10$, while Qwen-14B,
gain 3.1, does not cross by $g{=}20$). Gain is a rank-level \emph{screen}, not a sufficient
statistic; architecture residuals are real. The small-$g$ Qwen boost is also non-monotone in
scale (1.5B stronger than 3B/7B), which we leave unexplained.

location tested --- the two reference cells, the 8B scale cell, and the 14B

editor cell landing in the regime its layer's gain predicts. Restated over all \nCellsCombined{} cells
(ROME plus editor-varied), the ordering is Spearman(gain, constructive fraction)

robustness check rather than additional independent evidence, because the editor cells
revisit four model--layer locations already in the map and, by the thesis itself, gain is
a property of the layer. The independent-unit statistic remains the \nCells{}-cell
ordering of Section~\ref{sec:law} ($p=\ordSpearmanP{}$).

fraction \alphaEditConstrFrac{}, $\rho(\mathrm{dose},\mathrm{drop})=\alphaEditProjRho{}$), a
third editor in the low-gain regime. Second, editor choice dominates federation safety:
AlphaEdit's null-space projection, designed to protect preserved knowledge during sequential
editing, also suppresses federation cross-talk by up to an order of magnitude
(\alphaSuppressRange{} per-seed across cells and group sizes, three seeds; every seed
of every cell shows the same ordering) in both regimes at every group size. For a maintainer this is the
single largest safety lever in the map, ahead of group size and admission ordering. Third,
the safety lever inside MEMIT is the covariance, not the multi-layer spread. With
identity covariance, distributing an edit over several layers leaves damage at or
slightly above ROME's at matched cells, with an even stronger small-$g$ geometry
signal --- the spread alone does not shrink the interference surface. Replacing the
identity with an \emph{estimated} population covariance collapses federation damage
by 13--18$\times$ against identity-covariance MEMIT and 13--22$\times$ against ROME at
$g{=}2$ (3.5--4$\times$ at $g{=}20$; per-seed ranges, three seeds), while the cell
stays in the high-gain destructive regime (gain 17.7) and still passes the geometry
gate at every group size. The editor safety spectrum is thus FT $\to$ ROME $\approx$
MEMIT (identity) $\to$ MEMIT (est.\ cov) $\to$ AlphaEdit.

A fourth editor behaves as a boundary case. Adaptor-based editing that stores each edit in an
external discrete codebook, rather than in the weights, leaves the down-projection unchanged,
so its weight-space cross-talk is identically zero by construction~\cite{grace2023}. Weight
federation interference is therefore vacuous for that family: it sidesteps the phenomenon
this paper measures rather than falling in either regime, at the cost of carrying the
codebook at inference time. The two-regime law concerns editors that write into the shared
weight matrix.

\textbf{Dataset generality.} ROME federation on the zero-shot relation-extraction editing
task~\cite{decao2021} reproduces the CounterFact cells at both reference layers. At
Llama-1B L12 the zsRE cell passes with $g{=}2$ partials \zsrePartialsGtwo{}
tracking the CounterFact values \cfPartialsGtwoForZsre{} within seed noise at every group
size and matching damage magnitudes; at Qwen-1.5B L21 it passes with the same low-gain window
shape. The operating map is not an artifact of one fact distribution.

\section{A Gain-Screened Admission Framework}\label{sec:rule}
The map converts into a two-stage admission framework for a maintainer deciding which of $N$
candidate edits to co-deploy at a target layer.

\textbf{Deployment context.} The intended operator is whoever owns a deployed model
build --- a platform or knowledge-management team receiving edit requests from several
sources (product-fact corrections, entity updates, retractions) on a release cadence.
The admission gate runs once per release candidate, before any merge is committed. The
per-edit ordering uses only quantities available before deployment (each edit's stored
key and value vectors, via the closed form of Eq.~\ref{eq:crosstalk}). The layer screen
is a one-off calibration per model version and, unlike the ordering, is not vector-only:
gain is defined through observed logit drops, so estimating it requires forward passes
for a small calibration set of merges on a staging copy of the model
(\calibTime{} of GPU time per layer in our setting). Neither step adds serving-time cost
or probe traffic against the production model.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Screen the layer.} Estimate gain from a small calibration set of merges
built from the edits' own vectors and measured on a staging copy of the model
(\calibTime{} of GPU time per layer in our setting). High gain means the destructive regime: admit conservatively, geometry-ordered,
within the geometry-valid boundary ($g\leq5$ at the reference cell). Low gain means the
constructive small-$g$ regime --- but it also means edits install weakly there at all
(Section~\ref{sec:law}: esr collapses with gain), so the operational advice is not
``federate freely at low gain'' but ``do not place edits at low-gain layers; if
legacy edits already live there, small merge groups will not further damage them,''
with per-cell limits given by the boundary table of Section~\ref{sec:map}.
\item \textbf{Order candidates by predicted received interference.} Within a group each
edit's expected cross-talk is closed-form (Eq.~\ref{eq:crosstalk}); rank and cap candidates
by $I_{\cos}$. The same stored-vector quantities support a held-out per-edit damage
predictor (gate $\rho=\predictorRho{}$ at the reference layer) and a retrospective
capacity-constrained routing evaluation (oracle efficiency $\eta$: the total
collateral-damage reduction achieved by the geometry-signal router divided by the
oracle router's reduction at the same fixed memory budget; $\eta=\routingEta{}$,
\routingMargin{} over random, \routingSeeds{} seeds; retrospective and zero-parameter, with
no fitted thresholds).
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Operating window versus fleet scale.} We are explicit about a tension between
the motivation and the rule's validated window: a maintainer's backlog may hold
hundreds of candidate edits, while the geometry signal is valid only within small
merge groups ($g\leq5$ at the reference cell) and damage saturates by $g\geq20$. The
rule therefore governs \emph{how to compose small release batches}, not how to merge
a fleet in one step: large-fleet federation lives exactly in the saturated regime
where per-edit ordering no longer differentiates outcomes, and the practical
consequence of the map is that fleets should be released as sequences of screened
small batches rather than merged wholesale. Extending admission guidance into the
saturated large-$g$ regime is an open problem, not a gap this framework quietly covers.

Table~\ref{tab:benefit} evaluates the ordering step retrospectively under the frozen spec.
For each cell and each $g\leq5$, admission keeps the bottom-$q$ observations by $I_{\cos}$ at

\section{Falsified Intermediate Interpretations}\label{sec:falsified}
We report four intermediate interpretations that our own follow-up tests refuted; each
falsification sharpened the final claim. A \emph{single dose-continuum} was refuted by the
master-curve test --- bundles do not collapse at matched dimensionless dose, the same
relative perturbation produces \doseSpread{} different logit effects across models
(Section~\ref{sec:law}), with the layer's gain as the missing variable. A \emph{``sharp
7B$\to$14B jump''} proved an aggregation artifact of pooling over $g\leq20$, retired by
the $g$-resolved profiles of Section~\ref{sec:law}. A \emph{Qwen-specific} constructive
regime was refuted by Phi-3.5 (majority-constructive at its deep layer) and by GPT-2-XL
(the most extreme constructive cell, at 1.5B). And \emph{``gain is the carrier''} weakened
to ``gain is a rank-level screen'' under the matched-gain architecture residuals of
Section~\ref{sec:law}.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}
\textbf{Editor generality.} We demonstrate it at the two reference cells and, for the
destructive regime, at 8B scale (Section~\ref{sec:editors}: MEMIT-style and AlphaEdit,
pre-registered), and for the constructive regime at the deep 14B cell (AlphaEdit). The
remaining MEMIT gap has narrowed to the covariance source: our estimated-covariance
cell uses a held-out CounterFact prompt sample rather than a Wikipedia corpus, and we
name it ``MEMIT-style multi-layer spread'' throughout.
\textbf{Dataset.} CounterFact is primary, with a zsRE replication at both reference cells
(Section~\ref{sec:editors}); multi-hop~\cite{mquake2023} and ripple-effect~\cite{rippleedits2024}
evaluation streams, which probe knowledge downstream of an edit, are outside this paper's
scope.
\textbf{Scale ceiling.} The map spans 1--20B and, above 12B, more than one family: a
non-Qwen 12B cell (Mistral-Nemo) and a 20B cell (GPT-NeoX) join the 14B Qwen cell. The
held-out Llama-2-13B cell (Section~\ref{sec:law}) partly closes what was the sharpest
open anchor on this axis: it is a non-Qwen cell above 12B with clearly testable damage,
it reproduces the full two-regime trajectory, and its destructive tail passes the
geometry gate with a wide margin. It does not close the anchor outright --- it is a
single cell, its overall verdict is Mixed with one qualifying group size, it was run in
bf16 rather than fp32, and no directional prediction was frozen for it --- so a second
large non-Qwen cell with a pre-registered prediction and a qualifying window wider than
$\{20\}$ remains the natural next test.
\textbf{Retrospective evaluations.} The admission-benefit table and the routing efficiency
are retrospective; a confirmatory prospective arm is future work.
\textbf{Gain estimation.} Gain is measured here from merge observations; a cheaper edit-free
estimator from norm statistics is future work.
\textbf{Outcome metric.} We report target-logit drop on the edited fact; downstream and
global damage under federation are not re-measured per group size and remain future
work.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
The assumption that overlap between independently computed weight updates is
monotonically harmful is false for rank-one knowledge edits. Aligned cross-talk arrives
in every cell we measured, and its effect at the readout carries a sign that flips with a
measurable property of the layer: destructive, geometry-predicted over-amplification in
high-gain layers, and constructive small-group reinforcement with an
architecture-dependent crossover in low-gain layers. The regimes are depth-gated within
a model, are present from 1B to 20B and across \nFamilies{} architecture families, and
are ordered by one measurable scalar which a first-order readout model identifies as the
magnitude of the readout slope at the edit's operating point --- the regime being that
slope's sign. The account is a screen, not a sufficient statistic: architecture residuals
survive at matched gain, and the decisive test of the mechanism, reversing the sign of
the received dose within a cell, needs donor sets no natural edit bank supplies. Within
the two boundaries the map establishes --- geometry predicts damage only in small merge
groups, and the constructive regime largely coincides with layers where edits install
weakly --- the law converts into a gain-screened, geometry-ordered admission framework
for model maintenance, and it tells a maintainer in advance which layers that framework
will pay off at. For the science of merging it replaces a monotone assumption inherited
from task arithmetic with a measured, signed, and screenable one.
