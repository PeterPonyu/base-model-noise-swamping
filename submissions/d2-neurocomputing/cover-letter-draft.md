Dear Editors of Knowledge-Based Systems,

Please consider our manuscript, "When Do Rank-One Knowledge Edits Merge? A
Gain-Screened Two-Regime Law of Edit Federation," for publication as a research
article in Knowledge-Based Systems.

The paper addresses a knowledge-management problem that deployed language-model
systems now face routinely: many independently authored knowledge edits must be
merged into a single production model, and the maintainer must decide — before
committing the merge — which updates can safely coexist. We contribute the first
systematic operating map of this "edit federation" setting (22 model–layer cells
across seven architecture families, 1–20B parameters, under pre-registered
evaluation gates), an empirical two-regime interference law screened by a single
measurable quantity, and a deployable, auditable admission rule whose benefit we
quantify against random and magnitude-only baselines with an explicit ablation of
its knowledge component.

We believe the work fits the journal's scope directly: knowledge — its storage,
its maintenance, and decision support over its modification — is the protagonist
throughout, and the deliverable is an intelligent decision-support procedure for
knowledge-base upkeep in deployed neural systems, in the same problem family as
knowledge-editing work the journal has recently published. All evaluation gates,
thresholds, and directional predictions were frozen in writing before the
corresponding experiments ran, and the paper reports the intermediate hypotheses
our own follow-up tests refuted.

The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere. A companion mechanism-level
study of single-edit collateral damage is under review at a different journal and
is referred to in the text; the present paper's questions, experiments, and
contributions do not overlap with it. Code and result artifacts will be deposited
in a public repository with a DOI upon acceptance, per the journal's research-data
policy.

Thank you for your consideration.
