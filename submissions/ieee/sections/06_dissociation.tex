\section{Editor and Architecture Dissociation}
\label{sec:dissociation}

The law established in Sections~\ref{sec:regime}--\ref{sec:causal} is
\emph{locate-then-edit-mechanism-specific}, not a monotone function of how
much damage an editor does, and it is \emph{architecture-conditioned}
rather than universal. On a matched within-probe statistic (Llama-3.2-1B,
CounterFact, L8) six editors form a locality/coupling spectrum in which
coupling and mean damage move \emph{oppositely} (Table~\ref{tab:editorspectrum},
Figure~\ref{fig:editor}), anchored at the extreme-locality end by a codebook
editor whose weight delta is identically zero.

\subsection{Why fine-tuning is geometry-blind}
For full-rank fine-tuning (FT-L) the accumulated update is not rank-one:
Adam's per-coordinate rescaling $m_{ij}/\sqrt{v_{ij}}$ is not separable as
$f(i)g(j)$, so $\dW_{\text{FT}}$ has high effective rank and no row-space
direction aligned with $k$; $\dW_{\text{FT}}\cdot k_p$ does not factor
through $\cos(k,k_p)$ and the coupling is $\sim$0 (\ftRho{} at mean damage
\ftDmg{}, versus ROME's \romeRhoSzero{} at \romeDmg{}). KL-regularized
fine-tuning restores it partially (\ftkatRho{}, 3-seed, at mean damage
\ftkatDmg{}).

\subsection{KL-ladder dose-response}
Raising KL strength raises the coupling and lowers the damage; at 3 seeds
the coupling \emph{rises then plateaus} (reaching $\approx$0.15 by
kl~0.3, with kl~1.0 no longer exceeding kl~0.3), while the mean damage falls
monotonically, edit-success 1.0 at every rung (Table~\ref{tab:klladder}).
The dose-response is an \emph{L8} phenomenon: at L12 (the law's peak layer
for ROME) the same ladder is DEAD or borderline and non-monotone ---
within-probe $\rho$ \klLtwelveZeroThree{} / \klLtwelveOne{} /
\klLtwelveThree{} / \klLtwelveFull{} at kl~0.03/0.1/0.3/1.0 (single-seed),
all near or below the \deadThresh{} threshold --- so KL-FT's partial
restoration of geometry-predictability does not carry to the layer where
ROME's own coupling is strongest.

For MEMIT, the edit is spread across four layers, so a single-layer
rank-one identity does not describe it and the coupling is negligible at

\subsection{Architecture arm}
The \emph{signed} law is Llama-specific. Signed within-probe $\rho$, 3-seed:
gemma-2-2b~\citep{gemmateam2024gemma2} L13
\gemmaSigned{}\,$\pm$\,\gemmaSignedSD{}, Phi-3.5~\citep{abdin2024phi3} L16
\phiSigned{}, Qwen-0.5B~\citep{qwen2024qwen25} L12 \qwenHalfSigned{}
(borderline), Qwen-1.5B L14
\qwenFifteenSigned{}, Qwen-3B L18 \qwenThreeSigned{} (Figure~\ref{fig:crossarch}(a)).
Eq.~\ref{eq:sc} accounts for both breakdowns: near-null where $S\approx 0$
(distributed storage, gemma/Phi), inversion where the edit sits in a
net-improvement regime (Qwen). The Qwen inversion is norm-growth-clean,
scale-persistent, and causally geometry-carried (Section~\ref{sec:causal}).

\paragraph{The magnitude law transfers on four of five families.} The
unsigned magnitude law $|C|\rightarrow|\text{dmg}|$ is more portable
(Table~\ref{tab:magnitude}, Figure~\ref{fig:crossarch}(b)).
gemma is \emph{double-dead} --- geometry-blind on both the signed
(\gemmaSigned{}) and the magnitude (\magGemma{}, perm-$p$ \magGemmaPermP{},
n.s.) law --- which strengthens the anomaly rather than weakening the
transfer story. Phi's canonical magnitude is \magPhi{}, versus
$\approx$\magPhiPeek{} in an unfiltered estimate that omitted the
known-fact filter.\footnote{The known-fact filter restricts to probes the
base model knows ($\text{pre\_p}>0.05$). Without it, Phi's magnitude $\rho$
is $\approx$0.36. All headline numbers use both filters.}

\subsection{Depth dissociation on a second architecture}
\label{sec:gptjdepth}
GPT-J (6B, 28 transformer blocks) reproduces the coupling but relocates it in
depth and inverts its sign, a clean architecture$\times$depth dissociation.
Across its editable band the signed within-probe Spearman between key-cosine
and collateral damage is near-dead at the shallow layer and strong at the
deeper layers --- $\rho=\gptjRhoLfourteen{}/\gptjRhoLeighteen{}/%
\gptjRhoLtwentyone{}/\gptjRhoLtwentyfour{}$ at L14/L18/L21/L24 (3-seed means,
per-layer s.d.\ $\le 0.04$), tracked by the closed-form $S\!\times\!C$
statistic (\gptjScLfourteen{}/\gptjScLeighteen{}/\gptjScLtwentyone{}/%
\gptjScLtwentyfour{}). Two features distinguish GPT-J from Llama. First, the
coupling is \emph{negative} at every depth: like Qwen, GPT-J edits sit in the
net-improvement regime Eq.~\ref{eq:sc} predicts wherever $S$ is small and the
residual is improvement-directed. Second, its depth location is reversed ---
weakest at the shallow editable layer (L14, $|\rho|\approx 0.09$) and strongest
near the top (L24, $|\rho|\approx 0.35$), the opposite of Llama's mid-layer
(L12) peak. The geometry law's depth-of-action is therefore
architecture-conditioned, not a fixed fraction of network depth, and the causal
arm (Section~\ref{sec:causal}) confirms that geometry still predicts the
AlphaEdit-removable component on GPT-J.

\subsection{Edit-quality complement: the canonical EGL table}
\label{sec:egl}
The damage spectrum above describes what an editor perturbs in
\emph{unrelated} probes; the complementary view asks how well each editor
installs the intended edit. We report the canonical
Efficacy/Paraphrase/Neighborhood-specificity metrics for the three
weight-editors plus GRACE in text (Llama-3.2-1B, CounterFact, L12;
weight-editors 2-seed means, GRACE single-seed s0 --- indicative, not as
tightly bounded as the 3-seed damage statistics of this section).
All weight-editors install the edit ($\text{ES}\ge 0.995$) and preserve
paraphrases ($\text{PS}\ge\eglMemitPS{}$) but differ sharply on neighborhood
specificity --- the fraction of unrelated neighbors left unchanged: ROME barely
preserves neighbors (NS \eglRomeNS{}), MEMIT preserves most (\eglMemitNS{}),
AlphaEdit between (\eglAlphaNS{}) --- matching the mean-damage spectrum of
Table~\ref{tab:editorspectrum} and confirming the same locality ordering on the
standard edit-quality metrics.
% GRACE row source: egl_llama1b_grace_cf_L12_s0.json::EGL.{ES,PS,NS}
%   = 0.995 / 0.145 / 0.8565 ; single seed (s0). Constraint #7: only within-probe
%   gate + EGL are quotable for GRACE.
GRACE takes locality to its limit: it installs the edit as reliably
(ES \eglGraceES{}) while making no weight change, and it leaves neighbors
almost perfectly intact (NS \eglGraceNS{}, the highest of the six) but pays with
the worst paraphrase generalization by a wide margin (PS \eglGracePS{}) --- its
codebook fires only near the stored key, so the edit does not transport to
reworded queries. It thus occupies the extreme-locality, low-generalization
corner the weight-editor spectrum only approaches.
