\section{Generality: Datasets, Within-Family Scale, and a Sanity Model}
\label{sec:generality}

The core law relating pre-edit key-cosine to collateral damage
(\S\ref{sec:regime}) is established on CounterFact, the Llama-3.2-1B/3B/8B
family, and the editors profiled in \S\ref{sec:dissociation}. We stress it
along several independent axes --- none a headline on its own, each reported at
its true seed count and caveats --- detailed in the subsections below:
additional datasets (zsRE and the multi-hop MQuAKE / MQuAKE-T benchmarks), a
within-family scale replication, an instruction-tuned twin, a related-fact
(ripple) variant, a logit-to-GLUE downstream bridge, a hard-neighborhood
specificity probe, and a sub-threshold sanity model. Together they argue that
the law is not a CounterFact-, 1B-, or Llama-3.2-specific coincidence, and that
its geometric decomposition (the S$\times$C statistic of \S\ref{sec:regime})
continues to lead raw key-cosine at nearly every layer under these shifts, with
the single shallow-layer exception noted below.

\subsection{Dataset Generality: zsRE and Multi-Hop Benchmarks}
\label{sec:generality-zsre}
CounterFact and zsRE differ in how facts are phrased and how neighborhood
probes are constructed, so replicating the law on zsRE is the more direct
dataset-transfer test. On Llama-3.2-1B at L10, the within-probe Spearman
correlation between pre-edit key-cosine and collateral damage on zsRE is
\zsreSigned{}\,$\pm$\,\zsreSignedSD{} (signed) and \zsreMag{} (unsigned
magnitude), both at 3 seeds. These values sit in the same band as the
CounterFact law at the same layer (\S\ref{sec:regime}) and are not
attributable to any CounterFact-specific artifact in probe construction or
templated phrasing.

zsRE still shares CounterFact's single-hop, single-edit structure. The MQuAKE
benchmark and its temporally-scoped variant
MQuAKE-T~\citep{zhong2023mquake} instead package multi-hop
factual chains, a structurally different edit distribution.
Table~\ref{tab:datasetgen} reports the within-probe Spearman correlations at 3
seeds on Llama-1B/ROME. Because single-hop facts recur across multi-hop cases,
an edited fact often shares its subject and relation with one of the
neighborhood probes that enter the correlation: this holds for
% MQUAKE_overlap_audit.json::cells[mquake].frac_known_probes_colliding = 0.189..0.213
roughly one fifth of the entering probes on MQuAKE-CF and for the large
% MQUAKE_overlap_audit.json::cells[mquaket].frac_known_probes_colliding = 0.855..0.935
majority (86--94\%) of them on MQuAKE-T. The law survives this overlap:
excluding every colliding (edit, probe) pair leaves the correlations
essentially intact, with a shift of at most
% MQUAKE_overlap_audit.json::cells[mquake,L8,s0].rho_C = 0.3582 -> rho_C_nooverlap = 0.3520 (max, Delta 0.006)
$\approx$0.007 on MQuAKE-CF and
% MQUAKE_overlap_audit.json::cells[mquaket,L14,s0].rho_C = 0.2423 -> rho_C_nooverlap = 0.2037 (max, Delta 0.039)
$\approx$0.04 on MQuAKE-T
% MQUAKE_overlap_audit.json::cells[mquaket,L12,s0].rho_C = 0.4230 -> 0.3889; rho_SC = 0.5221 -> 0.4945
(e.g.\ at L12, key-cosine $\rho$ 0.423$\to$0.389 and S$\times$C $\rho$
0.522$\to$0.494), with no change in layer ordering or sign. On MQuAKE-T the
correlation holds even though nearly every entering probe shares a fact identity
with some edit, so the geometric predictor is not an artifact of overlapping
facts. On MQuAKE the geometric
decomposition (S$\times$C) leads raw key-cosine at all four profiled layers
% MQUAKE_mechanism_sc_table.json::groups[L12].within_probe_rho_SC = 0.6503
% MQUAKE_mechanism_sc_table.json::groups[L12].within_probe_rho_C  = 0.5945
(peak L12 \mquakeSCpeak{} vs \mquakeCpeak{}), reproducing the CounterFact
ordering and magnitude. On MQuAKE-T the same lead holds at L10--L14
% MQUAKET_mechanism_sc_table.json::groups[L12].within_probe_rho_SC = 0.5466
% MQUAKET_mechanism_sc_table.json::groups[L12].within_probe_rho_C  = 0.4493
(peak L12 \mquaketSCpeak{} vs \mquaketCpeak{}) but the two statistics
essentially coincide at the shallowest layer L8, where S$\times$C
% MQUAKET_mechanism_sc_table.json::groups[L8].within_probe_rho_SC = 0.2187
% MQUAKET_mechanism_sc_table.json::groups[L8].within_probe_rho_C  = 0.2305
(\mquaketLeightSC{}) sits a hundredth below key-cosine (\mquaketLeightC{}) ---
the one profiled cell where the decomposition does not add signal.

\subsection{Within-Family Scale: Llama-3.2-3B}
\label{sec:generality-scale}
The depth/regime law is profiled in full on Llama-3.2-1B; a natural question
is whether the law's specific layer-14 behavior --- where matrix-norm growth
overtakes key-cosine as the dominant correlate of damage
(\S\ref{sec:regime}) --- is a 1B-scale peculiarity. At the corresponding
depth on Llama-3.2-3B (L14), the within-probe correlation is
\llamaThreeBLfourteen{}, closely tracking the 1B reference at the same
relative depth (\llamaOneBLfourteenRef{}). This is a single-seed cell and we
report it as a replication at $3\times$ parameter count within the same
family, not as an independently seeded confirmation.

\subsection{A Sanity Model: GPT-2-XL}
\label{sec:generality-gpt2}
As a lower bound on model capability, we run the canonical
efficacy/paraphrase/neighborhood-specificity edit-quality metrics on
GPT-2-XL~\citep{radford2019gpt2} at two layers (L5/L17): Efficacy
\gptEsL{}/\gptEsH{}, Paraphrase \gptPsL{}/\gptPsH{}, Neighborhood-specificity
\gptNsL{}/\gptNsH{}. The paraphrase gap is a documented artifact of the
no-context paraphrase templates used at evaluation time, not a property of
the edit itself. \emph{Both cells have edit-success \gptEsrL{}/\gptEsrH{},
below our 0.9 screening threshold}, so we treat GPT-2-XL as a sanity check on
the harness and metric pipeline at a smaller, weaker base model --- not as a
headline generality result.

\subsection{Instruction-Tuned Twin}
\label{sec:generality-instruct}
Deployment models are typically instruction-tuned rather than base
checkpoints, so we repeat the CounterFact profile on the instruction-tuned
sibling --- same architecture and pre-training, plus supervised and preference
fine-tuning. Both the law and the S$\times$C-leads-at-all-layers ordering
survive at essentially base magnitudes (Table~\ref{tab:datasetgen}, 3 seeds):
the peak-layer S$\times$C correlation is
% INSTRUCT_mechanism_sc_table.json::groups[L12].within_probe_rho_SC = 0.6095
% INSTRUCT_mechanism_sc_table.json::groups[L12].within_probe_rho_C  = 0.5586
\instructSCpeak{} versus key-cosine \instructCpeak{} at L12, within a few
hundredths of the base-model reference, and the decomposition leads at all four
profiled layers including L14. Instruction tuning therefore does not erase the
geometric predictor.

\subsection{Related-Fact (Ripple) Damage}
\label{sec:generality-ripple}
The collateral damage measured so far is on facts unrelated to the edit. A
sharper question is whether the same geometry predicts damage to facts
logically \emph{related} to the edited one --- the ripple
effects~\citep{cohen2024ripple} an edit is meant to propagate. On Llama-1B/ROME (3 seeds) we measure, across depth, the
within-probe correlation between pre-edit key-cosine and ripple-set damage,
alongside the unrelated-collateral correlation on the same edits. The
predictability is non-monotone in depth: key-cosine tracks ripple damage with
% RIPPLE_depth_profile.json::profile[L8/L10/L14].rho_ripple_mean = 0.4539/0.4653/0.4915
$\rho\approx$~0.45--0.49 at L8, L10, and L14 but dips to \rippleLtwelve{} at
% RIPPLE_depth_profile.json::profile[L12].rho_ripple_mean = 0.2741
L12. The unrelated-collateral correlation follows the same depth shape
% RIPPLE_depth_profile.json::profile[*].rho_unrelated_mean = 0.4745/0.5455/0.3325/0.4492
(0.47/0.55/0.33/0.45) and slightly exceeds the ripple correlation at L8--L12,
% RIPPLE_depth_profile.json::profile[L14].rho_ripple_mean = 0.4915, rho_unrelated_mean = 0.4492
while at L14 the ripple correlation (\rippleLfourteen{}) overtakes it
(\rippleUnrelLfourteen{}). Geometry thus predicts related-fact damage about as
well as it predicts unrelated collateral, with the relative strength of the
two set by layer rather than by a fixed ordering.

\subsection{Downstream-Task Bridge}
\label{sec:generality-glue}
The damage metric so far is defined on next-token logits. To confirm these
perturbations matter for task behavior, we evaluate edited Llama-1B models on
three GLUE~\citep{wang2019glue} classification tasks (SST-2, MRPC, RTE),
measuring the change in
decision margin and the label-flip rate on held-out examples. Single edits do
move downstream behavior: at L12 the per-edit norm-growth --- the $S$ factor of
the decomposition --- predicts the magnitude of the margin
% GLUE_BRIDGE_summary.json::rows[gb_llama1b_{alpha,rome}_L12_*].ng_to_margin_damage_rho = 0.3426..0.5655
change with Spearman $\rho$ from \glueNGrhoLo{} to \glueNGrhoHi{} across seeds
and editors (all $p<10^{-6}$, 3 seeds), whereas the per-example key-cosine is
not predictive at this aggregated level. The editor ordering matches the
% GLUE_BRIDGE_summary.json::rome_vs_alpha_L12.mean_abs_margin_damage.ratio_rome_over_alpha = 5.56
% GLUE_BRIDGE_summary.json::rome_vs_alpha_L12.flip_rate.ratio_rome_over_alpha = 7.0
logit-level story: ROME inflicts \glueDmgRatio{}$\times$ the mean absolute
margin damage and \glueFlipRatio{}$\times$ the label-flip rate of AlphaEdit at
the same layer, though absolute flip rates stay small (roughly 3\% for ROME,
0.4\% for AlphaEdit). Logit-level collateral damage is therefore visible in
downstream decisions, and the geometric magnitude factor tracks its severity.

\subsection{Hard-Neighborhood Specificity}
\label{sec:generality-cfplus}
Standard neighborhood specificity is scored on comparatively easy neighbors. We
port a hard-neighborhood evaluation~\citep{hoelscher2023cfplus} ---
semantically close neighbors of the
edited subject --- and compare three editors at 3 seeds. On standard neighbors
the editors separate as expected, with neighborhood score \cfplusNSstdRome{}
% CFPLUS_aggregate.json::by_editor.{rome,alpha,memit}.NS_mean = 0.0817/0.4733/0.5700
for ROME, \cfplusNSmid{} for AlphaEdit, and \cfplusNSstdMemit{} for MEMIT. On
hard neighbors this separation collapses: every editor's neighborhood score
% CFPLUS_aggregate.json::by_editor.{memit,rome,alpha}.NS_hard_mean = 0.0133/0.0167/0.0183
falls to \cfplusNShardLo{}--\cfplusNShardHi{}, and the neighborhood KL rises to
% CFPLUS_aggregate.json::by_editor.{memit,alpha,rome}.KL_hard_mean = 6.2516/6.3808/6.5919
\cfplusKLhardLo{}--\cfplusKLhardHi{} nats regardless of editor. The specificity
advantage MEMIT and AlphaEdit hold over ROME on easy neighbors is thus largely
an artifact of neighbor difficulty; under semantically hard neighbors all three
editors are comparably disruptive.
