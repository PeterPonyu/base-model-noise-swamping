\section{Discussion, Practical Implications, and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

The results support a conditional claim: pre-edit key geometry predicts
collateral damage, causally, in a form --- signed or in magnitude, present or
inverted --- that depends on the editing mechanism and model family
(\S\ref{sec:regime}--\S\ref{sec:sequential}). We draw the practical
consequences, then the limitations.

\subsection{Practical Implications}
\label{sec:discussion-practical}
A natural use of a geometry--damage law is as a \emph{router}: use an edit's
pre-edit key geometry to choose an editing mechanism. This fails, a genuinely
negative result. The per-edit router is degenerate, always selecting the
null-space-projecting editor (AlphaEdit) regardless of input geometry, because
AlphaEdit's damage floor is low enough across the geometries we sampled that no
competing editor is ever preferred; geometry does \emph{not} predict which
editor to choose. What it \emph{does} predict robustly is how much an edit
benefits from that choice: the ROME-to-AlphaEdit damage ratio tracks the
coupling, ranging on the high-damage Llama regime from \dThreeRatioLten{}$\times$
(L10) to \dThreeRatioLfourteen{}$\times$ (L14), with L8 and L12 between
(\dThreeRatioLeight{}$\times$, \dThreeRatioLtwelve{}$\times$); near-zero-damage
regimes give unstable ratios. Geometry is thus a \emph{benefit-magnitude} signal
--- how much a damage-suppressing editor is worth deploying --- not a discrete
choice signal, reported qualitatively rather than as a tuned rule.

\paragraph{Choosing an edit layer is architecture-conditioned.} Neither the
editable band nor the safest depth within it transfers across families: the
band runs from Llama/Pythia's wide plateau to GPT-NeoX-20B's shallow cliff
(\S\ref{sec:regime}), and the coupling peaks mid-network on Llama but is
strongest near the top --- sign-inverted --- on GPT-J (\S\ref{sec:gptjdepth}).
A layer-selection heuristic calibrated on one family therefore cannot transfer
unchanged; the band and depth profiles of
\S\ref{sec:regime}--\S\ref{sec:dissociation} are the per-family calibration
a practitioner needs.

\paragraph{Scope of the strong causal law.} The removal test generalizes in
\emph{form} --- full strength on an instruction-tuned twin, sign-agnostic on a
second architecture, monotone at 20B (\S\ref{sec:causal}) --- but the
\emph{signed}, positive, large-coupling version is a small-to-mid-scale
statement: at 8B the signed causal coupling is weak and sign-unstable across
depth (\eightbCausalRhoLsixteen{} at L16, \eightbCausalRhoLtwentyfour{} at
L24), tracking the observational attenuation. We scope the strong
positive-signed law to $\lesssim$3B on current evidence, treating geometry at
larger scales as a magnitude cue about \emph{removable} damage, not a
sign-reliable predictor.

\paragraph{A zero-cost estimator, not a faithful influence surrogate.} The
worth of \SxC{} is its closed form: exact rank-one algebra, no gradient. It
does \emph{not} reproduce the rankings of the true first-order influence it
reduces --- within-probe rank agreement
% GRADSIM_TRUE_Llama-3.2-1B_L12_s0.json::rank_agreement.direct_vs_SC.mean = 0.0874
$\approx0.09$ against an explicit backward pass (\S\ref{sec:method}) --- a
distinction that matters wherever a faithful influence ranking is required.

\paragraph{From logits to task behavior.} The damage this paper measures is
defined on next-token logits, and a fair objection is whether such perturbations
matter downstream. On three GLUE tasks they do move behavior, and --- notably
--- what predicts the task-level margin change is the per-edit norm-growth (the
$S$ factor), not the per-example key-cosine, with Spearman $\rho$ from
\glueNGrhoLo{} to \glueNGrhoHi{} across seeds and editors (\S\ref{sec:generality}).
The two factors thus separate by level of analysis: the edit$\times$probe
cosine $C$ ranks damage \emph{across probes for a fixed edit}, while the
per-edit strength $S$ carries over to \emph{aggregate} downstream severity and
is the operative signal for triaging downstream-risky edits in bulk.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Scale.} Model scale caps at 20B, with the within-family
scale series verified at 1B/3B/8B; the coupling magnitude is \emph{attenuated
at 8B} ($|\rho|$ \regimeMagAttenLo{}--\regimeMagAttenHi{} vs.\ $0.38$ at 3B),
so we claim a sign-tracks-regime law, not a scale-invariant magnitude.

\paragraph{Architecture scope of the signed law.} The signed law is
Llama-family-specific: off Llama it goes null (gemma, Phi) or inverts (Qwen),
while the magnitude law transfers on four of five families, gemma the
exception (values in \S\ref{sec:dissociation}).

\paragraph{Mechanism scope.} The closed-form decomposition underlying our
core law is a single-layer rank-one ROME identity. It does not describe
full-rank fine-tuning (coupling $\sim$0) or MEMIT's multi-layer spread
(coupling DEAD at every layer); we report both as null arms rather than
force-fitting the algebra.

\paragraph{Remaining low-seed cells.} Single-seed cells, marked as such
in-text: MEMIT L10/L14 (\memitRhoLten{}/\memitRhoLfourteen{}); the KL-ladder at
L12 (\klLtwelveZeroThree{}/\klLtwelveOne{}/\klLtwelveThree{}/\klLtwelveFull{});
Llama-3B L14 (\llamaThreeBLfourteen{}); the zsRE-deletion transfer; the
generic-projector causal variant; and GPT-NeoX-20B among the
causal-generality cells of \S\ref{sec:causal} (the instruction-tuned twin,
GPT-J-6B, and Llama-8B cells are 3-seed). The canonical edit-quality metrics
(\S\ref{sec:dissociation}) are 2-seed and the sequential flank layers L8/L14
(\S\ref{sec:sequential}) are 2-stream; all other headline cells are 3-seed.

\paragraph{Anisotropy is descriptive.} The edit-key anisotropy
characterization (supplementary material) is key-space-only, with no damage
join and no causal claim. The Llama-vs-Qwen crowding contrast replicates
across 3 seeds, but the per-seed spectra remain a description of the edit-key
distribution, not evidence that anisotropy causes the regime.

\paragraph{Sequential geometry attribution is unsettled.} The sequential
section (\S\ref{sec:sequential}) is descriptive; the pre-registered
geometry-attribution gate is not passed (pooled partial \seqHone{},
$p{=}\seqHoneP{}$), and the survival result is ordering-dependent
(10--42\% range).

\paragraph{GPT-2-XL sanity.} Both GPT-2-XL sanity cells (\S\ref{sec:generality})
have edit-success \gptEsrL{}/\gptEsrH{}, below our 0.9 screening threshold; we
treat these as a sanity check, not a headline.
