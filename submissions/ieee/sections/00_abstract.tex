\begin{abstract}
Locate-then-edit knowledge editors such as rank-one model editing (ROME)
insert a fact by a rank-one update to one multilayer-perceptron (MLP) layer,
but that update also perturbs unrelated facts -- collateral damage. Our
central finding is that this damage is both predictable and causally removable
from geometry alone: on the Llama family, the cosine between an edit's key
vector and an unrelated probe's key predicts ROME collateral damage a priori
(confound-clean within-probe Spearman $\rho$ up to \gWithinLtwelve{} at layer
12), and null-space projection (AlphaEdit) causally removes that damage in
proportion to the same key-cosine, on models up to 3B parameters. The
prediction rests on a closed form: for rank-one ROME updates the collateral
perturbation factorizes exactly into a per-edit strength $S$, a per-probe norm,
and the edit$\times$probe key-cosine $C$, and the product \SxC{} is the exact
rank-one reduction of first-order gradient influence -- a zero-cost \emph{rank
estimator} needing no backward pass, though an explicit backward pass shows it
is not a faithful rank-surrogate of the true influence.

Two scope caps bound the account, and we state them up front. First, the
\emph{signed} form of the law is Llama-family-specific: the same-signed
geometry--damage coupling goes null on gemma and Phi and inverts on Qwen.
Second, causal confirmation is a small-to-mid-scale result: AlphaEdit's
geometry-proportional removal holds up to 3B but attenuates and can sign-flip
with depth at 8B, so the strong positive-signed causal law is not claimed at
larger scale.

Within those bounds the law dissociates cleanly. It undergoes a depth/regime
transition in which matrix-norm-growth overtakes the key-cosine at L14, and its
sign tracks the sign of the damage regime across model scale. It is
\emph{conditional on editor and architecture}: six editors form a locality
spectrum in which coupling and damage move oppositely, anchored at the extreme
by a codebook editor with zero weight change, and across six base models
spanning four architecture families (1B--20B parameters) the editable band
ranges from a wide plateau to a shallow-band cliff. The causal removal
further generalizes at full strength to an instruction-tuned twin and
sign-agnostically to a second architecture (both three-seed), and
monotonically at 20B (single-seed); the
law replicates across datasets, including the multi-hop MQuAKE benchmarks under
an overlap-robustness audit, and extends to deletion-style edits and
related-fact ripple damage. The account complements the concurrent empirical
predictor CLaRE-ty with the mechanism, the dissociations, and the causal test
it leaves open.
\iftaslp
Because locate-then-edit methods are increasingly proposed as a way to patch
factual errors in already-deployed language-technology systems, knowing in
advance when such a patch is safe to apply is itself a maintenance problem
for language processing pipelines, not only a modeling curiosity.
\fi
\end{abstract}
