\section{Anisotropy of the Edit-Key Distribution (Descriptive)}
\label{sec:anisotropy}

Two observations from \S\ref{sec:generality} and \S\ref{sec:dissociation}
remain unexplained at the level of raw geometry: why the norm-growth regime at
L14 is as pronounced as it is on Llama, and why Qwen's coupling inverts rather
than merely going null. This section characterizes the distribution of ROME edit
keys as a candidate --- but not asserted --- account of both.
\emph{This analysis is descriptive, key-space only --- no damage join, no
causal claim.}

\subsection{A Llama-vs-Qwen Crowding Contrast}
The central observation \emph{replicates across 3 seeds}: mean pairwise
key-cosine is \aniLlamaMeanCos{}/\aniLlamaMeanCosB{}/\aniLlamaMeanCosC{} on
Llama versus \aniQwenMeanCos{}/\aniQwenMeanCosB{}/\aniQwenMeanCosC{} on Qwen
--- Llama's edit keys occupy a much more crowded region of key space at the
same layer.

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Llama-1B L14 edit keys are strongly coned.} Mean pairwise
  key-cosine \aniLlamaMeanCos{}; the uncentered top-1 eigenvalue fraction is
  \aniLlamaTopOne{} (inflated by the shared mean direction, so we read it as
  ``keys share a dominant common axis,'' not a centered-covariance rank); the
  centered participation ratio \aniLlamaPR{} is far below both the
  column-permutation null ($\sim$\aniPermNull{}) and the norm-matched
  isotropic-Gaussian null ($\sim$\aniGaussNull{}). Such a cloud saturates the
  cross-edit interference term high with low variance, consistent with that
  term carrying the cross-edit damage variance at L14 (\S\ref{sec:regime}).
\item \emph{Qwen-1.5B L14 keys are much less coned} --- mean pairwise
  cosine \aniQwenMeanCos{}, uncentered top-1 \aniQwenTopOne{}, centered
  participation ratio \aniQwenPR{} --- a descriptive contrast with Llama at
  the same layer that co-occurs with Qwen's inverted coupling
  (\S\ref{sec:dissociation}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Scope of the Claim}
We do not join this characterization to collateral damage and make no causal
claim: the key banks here carry no damage matrices, and edit-key anisotropy is
confounded with tokenizer and subject-frequency effects we have not controlled
for. The crowding contrast is 3-seed; the top-1 and participation-ratio spectra
above are quoted at seed~0. The section thus offers a geometric description
consistent with, but not establishing, why Llama's L14 regime and Qwen's
inversion look the way they do.
