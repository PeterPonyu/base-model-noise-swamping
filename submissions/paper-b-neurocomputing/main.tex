\title{Edit Efficacy Survives 4-Bit Quantization but Damage Geometry Does Not:
Base-Model Noise Swamping in Knowledge-Edited Language Models}

\author{Zeyu Fu}
\cortext[cor]{Corresponding author.}
\ead{fuzeyu09@gmail.com}
\affiliation{organization={State Key Laboratory of Trauma and Chemical Poisoning,
Institute of Combined Injury, Chongqing Engineering Research Center for Nanomedicine,
College of Preventive Medicine, Army Medical University},
city={Chongqing}, country={China}}

\begin{abstract}
Knowledge edits are deployed into quantized models, so an operator needs to know what
survives the quantizer. Across three models (Llama-3.2-1B L12, Llama-3.2-3B L24,
Qwen-2.5-1.5B L21), three editors (ROME, MEMIT, AlphaEdit), three seeds and 27
model--editor--seed cells, we apply the bitsandbytes INT8 vectorwise and NF4
double-quant kernels to the edited MLP alone and to the full model, and measure two
things on the same edits: whether each edit still works, and whether the rank order of
edits by collateral damage is still readable. The two come apart. \emph{Efficacy
survives}: conditional on an edit succeeding in FP32 it still succeeds after
quantization with probability at least \pMinCondAll{} in all 36 arm-cells, above the
preregistered \pEsrSurvFourThreshold{} 4-bit threshold. \emph{The diagnostic value of
the damage geometry does not.} Full-model NF4 degrades cross-probe damage-rank survival
in an ordered way --- \pFlatNfFMLOneRome{} (Llama-1B), \pFlatNfFMLThreeRome{}
(Llama-3B), \pFlatNfFMQOneRome{} (Qwen-1.5B) under ROME, with the same ordering under
MEMIT and AlphaEdit --- clearing the preregistered \pRankSurvFourThreshold{} threshold
in only 1 of 9 cells, against 9 of 9 under edited-layer INT8. This ordering is not a
scale law: Qwen-2.5-1.5B has fewer parameters than Llama-3.2-3B and sits below it. The
mechanism is injected base-model noise, not distortion of the edit: \pAddShareNfFM{} of
the variance of the quantization residual under full-model NF4 is an edit-independent
per-probe offset correlating $r=\pAddRBaseNfFM{}$ with the independently measured
unedited-quantized baseline. Such an offset cancels within a probe column but reorders
probes against each other, which is the observed dissociation --- within-probe rank
holds at \pWithinNfFMQOneRome{}--\pWithinNfFMLOneRome{} for ROME where cross-probe rank
falls to \pFlatNfFMQOneRome{}. Post hoc, the ratio of injected base noise to
edit-attributable damage orders cross-probe rank survival across all 36 arm-cells at
Spearman $\rho=\pNsrRhoFlat{}$, against $\rho=\pNoiseRhoFlat{}$ for unnormalised noise
on the same outcome. Two consequences follow. Restricting the quantizer to the edited
layer and preferring INT8 both act by shrinking injected noise, and edited-layer INT8 is
the only arm preserving the diagnostic in all nine cells --- a deployment rule that
costs nothing to apply. And a paired hierarchical bootstrap ($n=\pBootNBoot{}$, paired
on edits) finds \emph{none} of \pBootNContrasts{} editor contrasts distinguishable at
95\%, so the editor ordering suggested by point estimates alone is not supported.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Edited language models are deployed quantized. INT8 weight quantization and 4-bit
double-quant are standard cost-control choices for serving
LLMs~\cite{hallucinationsurvey2023,kesurvey2023}, and rank-one knowledge edits
(ROME~\cite{rome2022}, MEMIT~\cite{memit2023}, AlphaEdit~\cite{alphaedit2025}) are
increasingly used to keep a deployed model's facts current without retraining. The
combination---edit then quantize---is therefore the realistic deployment path, and yet
editing and compression are usually evaluated in isolation. Where they are studied
together, the work focuses on absolute Strict-Edit-Locality F1 and reports that
quantization degrades locality. We do not replicate that F1 measurement here; it would
require the deployed-kernel protocol we place out of scope
(Section~\ref{sec:method-track15}).

This paper separates two questions that the literature tends to merge. A deployer needs
to know both whether an edit still \emph{works} after quantization and whether the
\emph{rank order} of edits by collateral damage is still readable, because that order is
what determines which edits to admit into the next release. Our central finding is that
these two properties dissociate sharply: efficacy survives everywhere we look, while the
diagnostic value of the damage geometry is destroyed under full-model 4-bit
quantization, in an ordered and mechanistically explained way.

We measure efficacy survival (conditional on FP32 succeeding, and absolute across the
full edit set) and damage-rank survival on the same edits installed before and after
quantization. The damage metric is the signed logit difference on held-out probes
disjoint from the edit set---never AUROC, which is a probe-marginal artifact in this
setting. Two rank estimands do the work. The \emph{cross-probe} rank
$\operatorname{Spearman}(D^{\mathrm{FP32}}.\mathrm{ravel}(), D^{q}.\mathrm{ravel}())$
pools the whole edit${\times}$probe grid and is therefore sensitive to anything that
shifts one probe column relative to another; the \emph{within-probe} rank, averaged
across non-degenerate probe columns, holds probe identity fixed and is immune to such
shifts. The contrast between them is what identifies the mechanism. We also report an
edit-level signed-mean rank and magnitude-sensitivity statistics, and the
\emph{base-quant-noise mean $|D|$} measured on an UNEDITED-quantized baseline arm---the
damage the quantizer injects with no edit installed, which turns out to be the governing
quantity.

\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{An ordered law.} Edit efficacy survives real INT8 and NF4 quantization in
every one of 36 arm-cells (conditional survival $\geq$\pMinCondAll{}), but cross-probe
damage-rank survival under full-model NF4 falls in a fixed order across models
---\pFlatNfFMLOneRome{}, \pFlatNfFMLThreeRome{}, \pFlatNfFMQOneRome{} under ROME---and
the same ordering appears under MEMIT and AlphaEdit. Only 1 of 9 cells clears the
preregistered 4-bit rank threshold, against 9 of 9 under edited-layer INT8.
\item \textbf{A mechanism, verified rather than inferred.} The quantizer's effect on
measured damage is an edit-\emph{independent} per-probe offset:
\pAddShareNfFM{} of the residual variance under full-model NF4 is probe-level, and that
component correlates $r=\pAddRBaseNfFM{}$ with the separately measured unedited-quantized
baseline. Such an offset cancels within a probe column and reorders probes against each
other, predicting exactly the observed dissociation between the two rank estimands.
\item \textbf{A deployment rule that follows from the mechanism.} Both available levers
---restricting the quantizer to the edited layer, and preferring INT8 to NF4---act by
shrinking the injected base noise, and edited-layer INT8 preserves the damage ranking in
all nine cells. Scope matters more than precision.
\item \textbf{A negative result on editor ordering.} A paired hierarchical bootstrap
finds none of \pBootNContrasts{} editor contrasts distinguishable at 95\%. Editor
rankings read off point estimates in this setting are not supported by the data.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Codec round-trip vs.\ packed deployment inference.} This paper's Track-1
measurement is the codec round-trip: install the edit in FP32, run
bitsandbytes \texttt{BitsAndBytesWeightQuantizer.quantize}/\texttt{dequantize}
(or the linear-modules \texttt{Linear8bitLt}/4-bit wrappers) on the edited MLP
or on every block linear, and re-measure against the same edit in FP32. This is
\emph{not} the packed-deployment-inference protocol of model-level quantization
followed by forward pass on the packed weights; that protocol is the deployed-kernel
packed-deployment protocol, which we do not run. The codec round-trip isolates the quantization
kernel's effect on the edit; the packed-deployment protocol adds the inference-stack
cost on top of it. We report the codec-round-trip numbers in this submission.

\textbf{A competing hypothesis, tested and rejected.} Before arriving at the
base-noise account we tested the natural alternative, ported from the
unlearning$\times$quantization literature: that concentrated parametric edits survive
quantization because their per-parameter changes $|\Delta W|$ sit at or above the
quantization bin width $b$, while diffuse gradient-style updates sit far below it. On
the axis we can measure, this hypothesis is dead. The fraction of edited parameters
above the bin width is \pKthreeFaboveMin{}--\pKthreeFaboveMax{} on the NF4 cells---three
orders of magnitude too small to carry an effect---so there is no concentration for
survival to depend on (Section~\ref{sec:c3}). This is what redirected the analysis from
the edit's own parameters to what the quantizer does to everything else.

We evaluate on three models (Llama-3.2-1B L12, Llama-3.2-3B L24, Qwen-2.5-1.5B L21),
three editors (ROME, MEMIT, AlphaEdit), three seeds, and two real quantization schemes
(bitsandbytes INT8 vectorwise and NF4 double-quant). Each cell produces four arms:
edited-tensor-only and full-model quantization at each precision. Twenty-seven (model,
editor, seed) cells in total, aggregated to nine (model, editor, layer) cells per
scheme. The preregistered rank-survival threshold at 4-bit full-model fails at two of
three models; rather than narrow the claim to the surviving cell, we report that failure
as the finding and characterise what replaces the signal. Scope, provenance and
preregistration status are consolidated in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Knowledge editing.} ROME~\cite{rome2022} and MEMIT~\cite{memit2023} established
the locate-then-edit pipeline that installs a rank-one update at a single mid-layer
MLP to add or correct a factual association. AlphaEdit~\cite{alphaedit2025} projects
the update onto the null space of preserved knowledge to suppress collateral damage
during sequential editing. Concurrent lines store edits externally rather than in the
weights: hypernetwork editors (MEND~\cite{mend2022}), retrieval-based memory
(SERAC~\cite{serac2022}), discrete key-value adaptors (GRACE~\cite{grace2023}), and
side-memory methods (WISE~\cite{wise2024}); PMET~\cite{pmet2024} sharpens the FFN
editing operation. Survey treatments~\cite{kesurvey2023,knowedit2024} taxonomize the
landscape. We restrict the present study to parametric editors (ROME, MEMIT,
AlphaEdit) because the descriptive reconstruction-gap signature is a property of
weight-space updates.

\textbf{Quantization of LLMs.} INT8 vectorwise~\cite{llmint82022} and 4-bit
double-quant NF4~\cite{qlora2023} are the two production-grade schemes shipped by
the bitsandbytes library and used here as the quantization kernel; GPTQ and AWQ are
popular alternatives we do not evaluate
(Section~\ref{sec:method-track15}). The damage-survival protocol is kernel-
agnostic in principle but the kernel choice matters: it controls the bin-width
statistic and the per-block outlier handling. We use the bitsandbytes reference
implementation, not a simulated-noise round-trip, because the bin-width hypothesis is
empirical and only meaningful under the actual kernel.

\textbf{Editing under compression.} Composable Interventions~\cite{composable2024}
measures absolute Strict-Edit-Locality F1 and reports degradation after quantization.
We report relative rank quantities instead, and on a codec round-trip rather than a
deployed kernel, so the two measurements are complementary rather than comparable;
running them on the same edits is left as future work
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). Our base-noise account does suggest what such a
comparison would find, since an edit-independent per-probe offset degrades absolute
locality scores and cross-probe rankings through the same channel.

\textbf{Unlearning $\times$ quantization.} Quantization can restore behavior that
full-precision unlearning appeared to suppress~\cite{quantunlearningfailure2025}.
Recent circuit-attribution work instead targets quantization-permanent
unlearning~\cite{forgettingsticks2026}. The bin-width hypothesis is ported from this
literature, where gradient-based unlearning produces a diffuse $\Delta W$ that
quantization erases and circuit-targeted updates are designed to persist. We port the
measurement (\S\ref{sec:method}) but not the unlearning setting; the present paper is
about editing, not unlearning, and the descriptive reconstruction-gap signature we
report is distinct from the concentration hypothesis we set out to test.

\textbf{Editing side effects.} Editing can fail in many ways beyond the local
F1~\cite{pitfalls2024}; few-edit-collapse~\cite{butterfly2024} and
gradual/catastrophic forgetting under sequential edits~\cite{seqforget2024} document
the broader fragility. Our relative-measure framing is complementary: it asks which
edits fail first, not whether the edit method fails in aggregate.

\section{Claims and specified design}
\label{sec:claims}

We specified three directional claims before any Track-1 Phase-1 run and evaluate them
against the nine-cell aggregate readout. The specified thresholds are listed in
Table~\ref{tab:track1}; gate outcomes are listed in Table~\ref{tab:gates}. One of the
three (C2) fails at two of three models, and that failure is the finding this paper
reports.

\textbf{C1 (efficacy survival).} ROME retains edit success with conditional probability
$\geq$\pEsrSurvEightThreshold{} at 8-bit and $\geq$\pEsrSurvFourThreshold{} at 4-bit on
the preregistered primary models Llama-3.2-1B and Qwen-2.5-1.5B. The stronger
prediction that MEMIT and AlphaEdit are no less robust than ROME is evaluated
separately. \textbf{Outcome}: the threshold holds --- not merely for ROME on the primary
models but for every editor in all 36 arm-cells, the lowest being \pMinCondAll{} (Qwen
AlphaEdit, NF4 full-model). The no-less-robust ordering is \emph{not decidable} from
these data: a paired hierarchical bootstrap on the editor contrasts leaves every one of
\pBootNContrasts{} comparisons statistically indistinguishable (Section~\ref{sec:c12}),
so we neither affirm nor reject the ordering. Llama-3.2-3B is the mandatory C2 extension
and is reported separately.

\textbf{C2 (geometric damage-ranking survival).} \textit{Where the FP32 law validates}
($\rho_{\mathrm{fp32}}^{\mathrm{within}} \geq$\pFpThreshold{}, established in-cell),
the Spearman between key-cosine and signed damage stays within $\pm$\pDeltaRhoTolerance{}
of the FP32 value. Damage-rank survival must be $\geq$\pRankSurvEightThreshold{} under
INT8 and edited-layer quantization and $\geq$\pRankSurvFourThreshold{} under 4-bit
full-model quantization. \textbf{Outcome: fails at 4-bit full-model}, and the pattern of
failure is the paper's result. Both validated ROME cells preserve the ranking under INT8
and edited-layer NF4. Under full-model NF4, Llama-3.2-1B L12 passes
(\pPrimarynfFourdqFullModelRankSurv{}) while Llama-3.2-3B L24 fails
(\pSecondnfFourdqFullModelRankSurv{} against \pRankSurvFourThreshold{}), and the
Qwen-2.5-1.5B cell is lower still (\pFlatNfFMQOneRome{}). Extending the readout past
C2's preregistered ROME-only scope, the same ordering across models holds under MEMIT and
AlphaEdit, and only \pNPassNfFM{} of 9 cells clears the threshold at 4-bit full-model.
Section~\ref{sec:swamping} shows this is one law, not three failures.

\textbf{Rank-survival definition.} The C2 number reported in
Table~\ref{tab:track1} is the \emph{flattened-grid rank}, computed as
$\operatorname{Spearman}(D^{\mathrm{FP32}}.\mathrm{ravel}(),
D^{q}.\mathrm{ravel}())$ over the held-out probe set for the cells pooled across
seeds. The v1.2.1 repair
artefact (n\_boot = \pRepairNBoot{},
hierarchical bootstrap seeds-then-edits) supplies three further per-arm
multilevel rank-survival statistics: a \emph{within-probe} rank (cross-edit
Spearman averaged within each non-degenerate probe column), an \emph{edit-level
signed-mean} rank (per-edit Spearman of $\overline{D}^{\mathrm{FP32}}$ vs
$\overline{D}^{q}$ across edits), and magnitude-sensitivity statistics
(\emph{edit-level absmean}, \emph{edit-level L2}, and the
\emph{base-quant-noise mean $|D|$} under the UNEDITED-quantized baseline arm).
The flattened-grid rank, within-probe rank, and edit-level signed-mean rank
together let us distinguish the regime on which the ranking is preserved; the
edit-level signed-mean is the closest operational proxy for the deployer's
question because it pools damage to the per-edit level before taking Spearman.
The repair artefact is the source for every operational number cited in this
paper.

\textbf{C3 (bin-width mechanism).} M-concentration predicts that edits survive
quantization when their per-parameter changes sit at or above the bin width $b$.
\textbf{Outcome: killed.} The fraction of edited parameters above the bin width is
\pKthreeFaboveMin{}--\pKthreeFaboveMax{} on the NF4 cells and the median $|\Delta W|/b$
is $\approx 0.03$; the edit is essentially entirely sub-bin-width, leaving no
concentration variation for survival to track (Section~\ref{sec:c3}). The mechanism this
paper reports instead is base-noise swamping (Section~\ref{sec:swamping}), which is a
property of the \emph{unedited} weights the quantizer also touches.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Track 1: geometry and mechanism (per-edit isolated)}
\label{sec:method-track1}

The isolated per-edit protocol installs edit $i$ in FP32, measures the per-edit
edit-success rate (esr) and the per-probe signed damage $\Delta\ell = \ell_{\mathrm{pre}}
- \ell_{\mathrm{post}}$ on a held-out probe set disjoint from the edit set, then
quantizes the edited MLP down-projection alone (``edited-layer'' arm) or every block
linear in the model (``full-model'' arm) under one of two schemes: INT8 vectorwise
or NF4 double-quant (NF4 with double-quant enabled), both via the bitsandbytes
reference implementation (INT8: per-row absmax with outlier dimension; NF4: 4-bit
normal-float with a per-block double-quant scale). The FP32 weights are not reverted;
the comparison is the FP32 arm vs the post-quantization arm on the same edit. Each
cell uses $n = 200$ edits, $m = 200$ held-out probes, seeds $\{0, 1, 2\}$.

\textbf{Codec round-trip, not packed deployment inference.} The Track-1
measurement is a quantize/dequantize round-trip on the affected tensors under
the bitsandbytes reference kernels, followed by a re-forward in FP32 on the
dequantized weights. This is not the same protocol as the packed-deployment
inference of a fully-quantized model (e.g.\ \texttt{model.quantize()} followed
by \texttt{model.forward()} on the packed weights), which is the deployed-kernel
packed-deployment protocol and is out of scope here. We isolate the quantization
kernel's effect on the edit; packed deployment adds the inference-stack cost on top.

The full-model arm uses a cache optimization: non-edited block linears are quantized
once per scheme; only the edited tensor is re-quantized per edit. This keeps the
per-edit wall time tractable without changing the kernel.

\textbf{Damage metric.} The damage metric is signed within-probe
$\Delta\ell_{\mathrm{logit}}$ on probes from a single edit's holdout set, i.e.\
$\ell_{\mathrm{pre},\mathrm{fp32\,unedited}} - \ell_{\mathrm{post},\mathrm{arm}}$ with
the sign convention that positive damage corresponds to a decrease in the target logit
under the arm. The bin-width statistic uses a coarser absolute-value proxy (Section~
\ref{sec:c3}). AUROC is not used as a damage metric; the within-probe Spearman is the
ranking quantity.

\textbf{Editors.} ROME is run with FP32 value optimization (no FP16 cast of the value
optimization; fp16 silently NaNs). MEMIT and AlphaEdit are FP32 by default. MEMIT's
estimated covariance uses a held-out CounterFact prompt sample, not a Wikipedia
corpus; the cell is named MEMIT-style multi-layer spread throughout.

\textbf{Generation checks.} A fixed slice of $n = 40$ generation probes (neutral
text continuations and two paraphrase probes per edit) is scored before and after
quantization; the per-arm mean perplexity on the fixed neutral text and the
paraphrase edit-success rate are reported. The generation slice is held fixed
across arms within a cell but is independent of the held-out damage probes.

\subsection{Kernels and protocols not evaluated here}
\label{sec:method-track15}

Two extensions are outside this study's scope and are noted so the boundary is explicit.
GPTQ and AWQ are not evaluated: a frozen-scale variant would calibrate once on the base
model, cache group scales, then re-round only the edited tensor per edit, but those
kernels are outside the evaluated software stack. And we do not run the batch-deployment
protocol---install all $n = 200$ edits, quantize the whole model once, then measure
aggregate efficacy, damage drift, and Strict-Edit-Locality
F1~\cite{composable2024} under both edit-then-quantize and quantize-then-edit
orderings. Both are natural follow-ups (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}); neither is
claimed or reported.

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}

\textbf{Damage.} For probe $j$ under edit $i$,
$D_{ij}=\ell^{\mathrm{pre}}_j-\ell^{\mathrm{post}}_{ij}$, so positive values denote a
decrease in the correct-token logit; AUROC is excluded. \textbf{Geometry.}
$G_{ij}=\cos(k_i,k_j^{\mathrm{probe}})$, and the within-probe statistic averages
$\operatorname{Spearman}_i(G_{ij},D_{ij})$ across non-degenerate probe columns $j$.
\textbf{Cross-probe rank survival.} Spearman correlation between the flattened FP32 and
quantized damage matrices,
$\operatorname{Spearman}(D^{\mathrm{FP32}}.\mathrm{ravel}(),
D^{q}.\mathrm{ravel}())$. Because it pools the whole edit${\times}$probe grid it
compares damage across probes as well as across edits, and is therefore sensitive to
anything that shifts one probe column relative to another --- the property that makes
its contrast with the within-probe statistic informative. The C2 numbers in
Table~\ref{tab:track1} are this cross-probe rank. \textbf{Operational edit ranking.}
The per-edit ranking a deployer would consume---Spearman between the per-edit
FP32 damage vector and the per-edit quantized damage vector, indexed by edit
rather than by the flattened probe grid---is reported in the v1.2.1 repair
artefact as the edit-level signed-mean rank; this paper uses those values
verbatim and does not invent any operational number.
\textbf{Efficacy survival.} The post-quantization edit success rate conditioned
on the corresponding FP32 edit succeeding. \textbf{Absolute mean ESR.} The
post-quantization edit success rate on the full edit set (including edits that
did not succeed in FP32); reported alongside conditional survival so that the
absolute floor of the protocol is visible. \textbf{Permutation null.} $N \geq
1000$ shuffles of geometry relative to damage. \textbf{Uncertainty.} Bootstrap
95\% intervals resample edits within each seed before aggregation across the
three seeds.

\textbf{Editor ordering is not resolvable from these data.} The point estimates in
Table~\ref{tab:esr-grid} appear to order the editors on the Qwen arms, and an earlier
draft of this work read that ordering as a failure of the C1 no-less-robust prediction.

removes the edit-sampling variance common to them. Under that test, \emph{none} of the
\pBootNContrasts{} pairwise contrasts is distinguishable from zero at 95\%. On Qwen NF4
full-model, the arm where the apparent gap is largest, ROME minus MEMIT is
\pBootQwenNfFMRomeMemitD{} (CI95 [\pBootQwenNfFMRomeMemitLo{},
\pBootQwenNfFMRomeMemitHi{}], $p=\pBootQwenNfFMRomeMemitP{}$), ROME minus AlphaEdit is

\pBootQwenNfFMRomeAlphaHi{}], $p=\pBootQwenNfFMRomeAlphaP{}$), and MEMIT minus AlphaEdit

differences larger than roughly ten percentage points would have been detectable; this
is an indecisive result, not a demonstration of equivalence.

\subsection{Base-noise swamping}
\label{sec:swamping}

Everything in this subsection is \textbf{post hoc and exploratory}. None of it was
preregistered; it is a mechanism account constructed after a preregistered gate failed,
and it should be read as a hypothesis that the data are consistent with rather than as a
tested prediction. We state it because it explains all nine cells with one quantity and
because it implies a deployment rule that can be checked cheaply by anyone.

\textbf{The quantizer's effect on measured damage is edit-independent.} Write the
residual $R = D^{q} - D^{\mathrm{FP32}}$, the change quantization makes to the damage
matrix. If quantization distorted the \emph{edit}, $R$ would vary with the edit index
$i$. It does not. Decomposing $R$ into its per-probe column mean and the remainder,
the column-mean component carries \pAddShareNfFM{} of the total variance under
full-model NF4 and \pAddShareIntFM{} under full-model INT8; and that component
correlates $r=\pAddRBaseNfFM{}$ and $r=\pAddRBaseIntFM{}$ respectively with
$\mathrm{base}[j]$, the damage measured on the UNEDITED-quantized baseline arm --- a
quantity recorded independently, with no edit installed at all. In other words
$D^{q}[i,j] \approx D^{\mathrm{FP32}}[i,j] + \mathrm{base}[j]$: the quantizer adds a
per-probe offset that the edit knows nothing about. Under edited-layer quantization,
where far less of the model is touched, the probe-level share drops to
\pAddShareNfEL{} (NF4) and \pAddShareIntEL{} (INT8).

We flag the weakness of that last check rather than lean on it. The prediction was
directional only --- less of the model quantized, less edit-independent offset --- with
no falsification band fixed in advance, so almost any decrease would have been read as
confirmation. It is also worth being explicit about an awkwardness the numbers create
for us: the deployment rule below recommends edited-layer INT8, which is precisely the
arm where the additive description fits \emph{worst} (probe-level share
\pAddShareIntEL{}, $r=\pAddRBaseIntEL{}$). That is not a contradiction --- the offset is
small there, so it explains little of a residual that is itself nearly nil --- but it
does mean the rule is not derived from the mechanism. The rule rests on the measured
survival numbers in Table~\ref{tab:rank-grid}, which hold whether or not a reader accepts
the additive account.

\textbf{This predicts the dissociation between the two rank estimands.} A per-probe
offset is a constant within probe column $j$, so it cancels exactly in a within-probe
Spearman; but it shifts whole columns relative to one another, which is precisely the
comparison the cross-probe Spearman makes. The prediction is that cross-probe rank
collapses while within-probe rank is spared, which is what
Table~\ref{tab:rank-grid} shows.

\textbf{One quantity orders all nine cells.} What should govern the collapse is not the
injected noise in absolute terms but its size relative to the signal it competes with.
Define the noise-to-signal ratio
\[
\mathrm{NSR} \;=\; \frac{\text{mean}\,|D|\ \text{on the unedited-quantized baseline}}
{\text{mean}\,|D^{\mathrm{FP32}}|\ \text{(edit-attributable damage)}} .
\]
Across all 36 arm-cells, $\operatorname{Spearman}(\mathrm{NSR}, \text{cross-probe rank
survival}) = \pNsrRhoFlat{}$ ($p=\pNsrRhoFlatP{}$); within the nine 4-bit full-model
cells alone the rank agreement is exact ($\rho=\pNsrRhoFlatNfFM{}$). The unnormalised
noise does much less work ($\rho=\pNoiseRhoFlat{}$ against cross-probe rank, and
$\rho=\pNoiseRhoCond{}$, $p=\pNoiseRhoCondP{}$, against conditional efficacy survival),
which is expected: under full-model NF4 the injected noise is nearly constant across
models (0.61--0.72) while the edit-attributable damage varies by two orders of
magnitude. The against-within-probe correlation is weaker still
($\rho=\pNsrRhoWithin{}$), consistent with the offset largely cancelling there.

Table~\ref{tab:nsr} lays out the nine 4-bit full-model cells. It also dissolves the
apparent editor effect. AlphaEdit sits at the bottom of every column not because it is
fragile under quantization but because it is designed to produce little collateral
damage in the first place: its mean $|D^{\mathrm{FP32}}|$ is \pSigLOneAlpha{} at
Llama-1B against \pSigLOneRome{} for ROME, so the same injected noise is comparatively
far larger. Low damage and low measurability are the same fact seen twice.

\textbf{What is and is not surprising here.} Given an additive-noise model, attenuation
of a rank correlation as noise grows relative to signal is an analytic consequence, not
a discovery --- it is classical attenuation by measurement error. The substantive
content is the antecedent: that the quantizer's effect on edit-damage measurement
\emph{is} well described as an edit-independent additive offset, with
\pAddShareNfFM{} of the residual variance and $r=\pAddRBaseNfFM{}$ against a separately
measured baseline. That is what licenses the deployment rule below, and it is not
something one could assume. A reader who rejects the additive description is free to
treat $\rho=\pNsrRhoFlat{}$ as a redescription of the failure rather than an explanation
of it; the deployment rule survives either reading, because it depends only on the
measured fact that less injected noise means more readable damage ranking.

\textbf{Deployment rule.} Two levers reduce injected base noise, and both preserve the
diagnostic. Restricting the quantizer to the edited layer instead of the whole model
raises the number of cells clearing the preregistered 4-bit rank threshold from
\pNPassNfFM{} of 9 to \pNPassNfEL{} of 9 at NF4; choosing INT8 over NF4 raises it from
\pNPassNfFM{} to \pNPassIntFM{} at full model. Applied together, edited-layer INT8
preserves the ranking in all \pNPassIntEL{} of 9 cells, with a worst case of
\pMinFlatIntEL{}. Scope is the stronger lever: full-model INT8
(worst case \pMinFlatIntFM{}) is less protective than edited-layer NF4
(worst case \pMinFlatNfEL{}) on these cells. Since a knowledge edit touches one MLP,
excluding that tensor from the quantizer costs a negligible fraction of the memory
saving, and it is what we would recommend to any deployer who intends to keep
monitoring which edits are doing damage.

\textbf{K3 is killed on the axis we can measure.} The concentration hypothesis needs
some appreciable mass of the edit to sit at or above the quantization bin width. It does
not. On the NF4 cells the fraction above bin width is $F_{\mathrm{above}} =
\pPrimaryNfFourDqFabove{}$ (Llama-3.2-1B L12 ROME) and $\pSecondNfFourDqFabove{}$
(Llama-3.2-3B L24 ROME), with median $|\Delta W|/b$ of
$\pPrimaryNfFourDqMedianRatio{}$ and $\pSecondNfFourDqMedianRatio{}$. Between one and
four parts in a thousand of the edited parameters clear the bin width; the edit is
effectively entirely sub-bin-width. There is no concentration variation for survival to
track, which is a dead predictor, not an inconclusive one.

That verdict is robust to the denominator question. These ratios use the readout's
local-gap denominator
$b=\operatorname{absmax}_{\mathrm{block}}\,\operatorname{local\_gap}(w/\operatorname{absmax}_{\mathrm{block}})$
(blocksize 64) rather than the channel-scale $\times$ NF4 minimum-gap convention the
study had intended. The substitution is a strictly positive per-element rescaling of
$b$: it can move $F_{\mathrm{above}}$ up or down in magnitude but cannot make a
predictor that is identically near zero into one that separates cells, because it
reorders nothing that the concentration hypothesis needs. We therefore adjudicate K3 as
\textbf{killed on the measured axis} rather than deferring it. What we do \emph{not}
claim is that the amended channel-scale axis has been measured: it has not, and a
concentration effect defined on some other rescaling remains formally untested. We do
not consider that a live prospect given the margin involved. INT8 is not decomposed into
outlier and bin-width components here, so its rows in Table~\ref{tab:c3} are descriptive.

The reconstruction-gap statistic in the same table is
$r_{\mathrm{func}}=\|\epsilon x\|/\|\Delta W x\|$, the relative reconstruction error on
the edit's key activation $x$, against
$r_{\mathrm{param}}=\operatorname{median}|\epsilon|/\operatorname{median}|\Delta W|$,
with $\epsilon = \mathrm{Q}(W{+}d) - (W{+}d)$. Because $\epsilon$ is the gap between the
quantized and unquantized \emph{edited} weight rather than a quantized-update error, it
summarises the total rounding budget and is not a mechanism claim. It is ordered
consistently ($r_{\mathrm{func}} < r_{\mathrm{param}}$ in all 18 scheme blocks; median
ratio $0.00570$ at INT8, $0.00591$ at NF4), and we report it as a descriptive property
of the codec. The mechanism this paper advances is in Section~\ref{sec:swamping} and
does not rest on these quantities.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{The central mechanism is post hoc.} The base-noise account of
Section~\ref{sec:swamping} was constructed after the preregistered rank-survival gate
failed, and none of its quantities --- the noise-to-signal ratio, the residual
decomposition, the associated correlations --- was specified in advance. It is a
hypothesis consistent with these 36 arm-cells, not a tested prediction, and the
attenuation it describes is analytically expected once one grants the additive
description. Its status as an explanation therefore rests on the additive description
itself being well supported, which is the one part we can point at directly
(\pAddShareNfFM{} of residual variance, $r=\pAddRBaseNfFM{}$ against an independently
measured baseline). A prospective test --- predicting rank survival on an unseen
model$\times$arm from its noise-to-signal ratio alone --- is the obvious next step and
has not been run.

\textbf{Preregistration status and one divergence, stated plainly.} The preregistration
document was prepared as a DRAFT and was not ratified as active before any cell ran; its
file mtimes fall after several real cells, and cell runs proceeded under the project's
house pre-specification convention. The core thresholds (C1 efficacy survival
$\geq 0.90$ at 8-bit, $\geq 0.80$ at 4-bit; C2 $|\Delta\rho| \leq 0.15$ with rank
survival $\geq 0.95$ at 8-bit and $\geq 0.85$ at 4-bit full-model; K1--K3) were fixed
prospectively in the design document. Two protocol-level entries in the DRAFT --- the
K3 bin-width denominator and the Strict-Edit-Locality F1 definition --- cannot be
chronologically classified as pre- or post-data, and we do not classify them.

The substantive divergence is this: the preregistered cross-probe rank-survival
threshold \emph{failed} at 4-bit full-model on two of three models, and this paper's
headline is built on the pattern of that failure rather than on any surviving cell. We
report it this way because retreating to the one cell that passed would have hidden the
finding. Readers who prefer the strict preregistered reading should take the paper as
reporting a failed gate plus an unregistered mechanism hypothesis for why it failed;
that reading is correct and is the reason the whole of
Section~\ref{sec:swamping} is labelled exploratory. We note also that C2 was
preregistered ROME-only; the MEMIT and AlphaEdit cells that make the ordering look like
a law rather than an accident are outside that scope and are reported as extensions.

\textbf{Scope of the measurement.} Three models, one layer each, 200 edits and 200
held-out probes per cell, and one edit installed at a time --- we do not measure batch
deployment (all 200 edits installed, then a single whole-model quantization), which can
behave differently. The quantization is a bitsandbytes codec round-trip followed by an
FP32 re-forward on dequantized weights, not packed-kernel inference; GPTQ and AWQ are
not evaluated at all, and kernel choice affects both the bin-width statistic and
per-block outlier handling. The Qwen-2.5-1.5B FP32 within-probe law does not clear the
in-cell gate, so that cell gates no preregistered claim, and its low absolute ESR floor
(Table~\ref{tab:absolutes}) widens every edit-level interval computed on it. Llama-3.1-8B
was dropped from the grid: its FP32 within-probe law is dead at the tested layer, so it
could not have supplied a C2 verdict, and with no measurable edit-attributable damage
signal it also could not contribute a usable noise-to-signal point to
Section~\ref{sec:swamping} --- the denominator is what the cell lacks. Its efficacy
(C1) numbers would have been reportable, and their omission is a real if minor gap.
Extrapolation past these arm--cell combinations is not warranted.

\textbf{Reproducibility.} All numbers in this paper derive from three analysis
artefacts computed over the same 27 per-cell raw arrays. The first is the canonical
v\pRepairVersion{} readout (hierarchical bootstrap, seeds-then-edits,
$n_{\mathrm{boot}}=\pRepairNBoot{}$), which supplies the efficacy, rank-survival and
bin-width quantities in Tables~\ref{tab:track1}--\ref{tab:c3} and the gate statuses in
Table~\ref{tab:gates}. The second is the paired editor-ordering bootstrap
($n=\pBootNBoot{}$, rng seed 12345), which supplies every contrast in
Section~\ref{sec:c12}. The third is the base-noise-swamping analysis, which supplies
Table~\ref{tab:nsr} and the residual decompositions and correlations of
Section~\ref{sec:swamping}. The latter two were added in revision and each runs on CPU
in about a minute; both are deterministic under their recorded seed and emit a JSON
carrying estimand definitions, bootstrap specification and per-cell values, from which
the manuscript's numeric macros are generated rather than transcribed. The three
artefacts, the two analysis scripts and the per-cell arrays are released together, so
each reported figure can be traced to the file and code path that produced it.
Generation checks ($n=40$ prompts) are per-seed point estimates, are not bootstrapped,
and gate nothing.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Edit efficacy and edit \emph{diagnosability} do not survive quantization together. Under
real bitsandbytes kernels, edits keep working: conditional efficacy survival is at least
\pMinCondAll{} in all 36 arm-cells, comfortably above the preregistered
\pEsrSurvFourThreshold{} 4-bit threshold, and at least 0.978 in every INT8 arm. But the
ability to tell which edits are doing the most collateral damage --- the quantity a
deployer needs in order to decide what to ship --- degrades sharply under full-model
4-bit quantization, and in a fixed order: cross-probe rank survival is
\pFlatNfFMLOneRome{} at Llama-3.2-1B L12, \pFlatNfFMLThreeRome{} at Llama-3.2-3B L24 and
\pFlatNfFMQOneRome{} at Qwen-2.5-1.5B L21 under ROME, with the same ordering under MEMIT
and AlphaEdit. Only \pNPassNfFM{} of 9 cells clears the preregistered
\pRankSurvFourThreshold{} threshold there, against \pNPassIntEL{} of 9 under
edited-layer INT8. The preregistered gate failed, and its pattern of failure is what
this paper reports.

The mechanism, offered post hoc, is that the quantizer injects a per-probe offset that
the edit knows nothing about. \pAddShareNfFM{} of the variance of the quantization
residual under full-model NF4 is edit-independent and probe-level, correlating
$r=\pAddRBaseNfFM{}$ with damage measured on an unedited quantized model. Such an offset
cancels within a probe column but reorders columns against each other, which is why
within-probe rank holds at \pWithinNfFMQOneRome{}--\pWithinNfFMLOneRome{} on the cells
where cross-probe rank has fallen to \pFlatNfFMQOneRome{}. Normalising the injected
noise by the edit's own damage magnitude orders every one of the 36 arm-cells
($\rho=\pNsrRhoFlat{}$), and it dissolves the apparent editor effect: AlphaEdit measures
worst because it damages least, not because it is fragile. The competing bin-width
concentration hypothesis is dead on the axis we can measure --- between
\pKthreeFaboveMin{} and \pKthreeFaboveMax{} of edited parameters clear the bin width, so
there is no concentration for survival to track.

Two things follow for practice. Restricting the quantizer to the edited layer, and
preferring INT8, both shrink the injected noise; together they preserve the damage
ranking in all nine cells with a worst case of \pMinFlatIntEL{}. Excluding a single
edited MLP from quantization costs almost none of the memory saving, so a deployer who
wants to keep monitoring edit damage can have both. And editor comparisons in this
setting need uncertainty estimates before they are believed: a paired bootstrap leaves
all \pBootNContrasts{} contrasts indistinguishable, retracting an ordering claim that
point estimates alone had seemed to support.

The natural next tests are prospective. Whether a cell's noise-to-signal ratio predicts
its rank survival on unseen models and arms, and whether the same offset explains the
absolute Strict-Edit-Locality F1 degradation reported for editing under
compression~\cite{composable2024}, are both answerable with the protocol here extended
to a deployed kernel and a batch-deployment ordering.
