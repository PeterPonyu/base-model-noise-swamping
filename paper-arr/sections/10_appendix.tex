\section{Anisotropy of the Edit-Key Distribution}
\label{app:aniso}
\emph{Descriptive, key-space only --- no damage join, no causal claim.} We
characterize the distribution of ROME edit keys, offering a candidate reading
of the L14 regime and the Qwen behavior without asserting it causes them. The
Llama-vs-Qwen crowding contrast \emph{replicates across 3 seeds}: mean pairwise
key-cosine \aniLlamaMeanCos{}/\aniLlamaMeanCosB{}/\aniLlamaMeanCosC{} (Llama)
vs.\ \aniQwenMeanCos{}/\aniQwenMeanCosB{}/\aniQwenMeanCosC{} (Qwen).

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Llama-1B L14 edit keys are strongly coned.} Mean pairwise
  key-cosine \aniLlamaMeanCos{}; the uncentered spectrum's top-1 eigenvalue
  fraction is \aniLlamaTopOne{} (inflated by the shared mean direction, so read
  it as ``keys share a dominant common axis,'' not a centered-covariance rank);
  the centered participation ratio is \aniLlamaPR{}, far below the
  column-permutation null ($\sim$\aniPermNull{}) and the norm-matched
  isotropic-Gaussian null ($\sim$\aniGaussNull{}). A highly anisotropic key
  cloud means $|C|$ saturates high with low variance, consistent with $S$
  carrying the cross-edit damage variance at L14.
\item \textbf{Qwen-1.5B L14 keys are much less coned} (mean pairwise cosine
  \aniQwenMeanCos{}, uncentered top-1 \aniQwenTopOne{}, centered PR
  \aniQwenPR{}) --- a descriptive contrast with Llama at the same layer.
\end{itemize}
We do not join this to collateral damage and make no causal claim: the banks
carry no damage matrices, and edit-key anisotropy is confounded with
tokenizer / subject-frequency (3-seed crowding contrast; the top-1/PR spectra
are quoted at seed~0).

\section{Reframed Editor-Choice Routing}
\label{app:d3}
Geometry does \emph{not} predict editor choice: null-space routing is
degenerate (always-AlphaEdit). It predicts benefit \emph{magnitude}: the
ROME$\rightarrow$AlphaEdit damage ratio tracks coupling, largest on the
high-damage Llama regime (L8/L10/L12/L14 $\approx$ \dThreeRatioLeight{}$\times$
/ \dThreeRatioLten{}$\times$ / \dThreeRatioLtwelve{}$\times$ /
\dThreeRatioLfourteen{}$\times$), with near-zero-damage families giving
unstable/near-zero ratios. We report this qualitatively.
