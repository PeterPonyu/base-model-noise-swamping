\section{Editor and Architecture Dissociation}
\label{sec:dissociation}

The law is \textbf{locate-then-edit-mechanism-specific}, not a monotone
function of how much damage an editor does. On a matched within-probe statistic
(Llama-3.2-1B, CounterFact, L8) the editors form a locality/coupling spectrum
in which coupling and mean damage move \emph{oppositely}
(Table~\ref{tab:editorspectrum}, Figure~\ref{fig:editor}).

\paragraph{Why fine-tuning is geometry-blind.} For full-rank fine-tuning
(FT-L) the accumulated update is not rank-one: Adam's per-coordinate rescaling
$m_{ij}/\sqrt{v_{ij}}$ is not separable as $f(i)g(j)$, so $\dW_{\text{FT}}$ has
high effective rank and no row-space direction aligned with $k$;
$\dW_{\text{FT}}\cdot k_p$ does not factor through $\cos(k,k_p)$ and the
coupling is $\sim$0 (\ftRho{} at mean damage \ftDmg{}, vs.\ ROME
\romeRhoSzero{} at \romeDmg{}). KL-regularized FT restores it partially
(\ftkatRho{}, 3-seed, at mean damage \ftkatDmg{}).

\paragraph{KL-ladder dose-response.} Raising KL strength raises the coupling
and lowers the damage; at 3 seeds the coupling \emph{rises then plateaus}
(reaching $\approx$0.15 by kl~0.3, with kl~1.0 no longer exceeding kl~0.3),
while the mean damage falls monotonically, edit-success 1.0 at every rung
(Table~\ref{tab:klladder}). The dose-response is an \emph{L8} phenomenon: at
L12 (the law's peak layer for ROME) the same ladder is DEAD/borderline and
non-monotone --- within-probe $\rho$ \klLtwelveZeroThree{} / \klLtwelveOne{} /
\klLtwelveThree{} / \klLtwelveFull{} at kl~0.03/0.1/0.3/1.0 (single-seed), all
near or below the \deadThresh{} threshold --- so KL-FT's partial restoration of
geometry-predictability does not carry to the layer where ROME's own coupling
is strongest.

\paragraph{MEMIT.} For MEMIT, the edit is spread across four layers, so a
single-layer rank-one identity does not describe it and the coupling is
negligible at every layer.

For MEMIT, the within-probe Spearman between key-cosine $|C|$ and collateral
damage is negligible --- \memitRhoLeight{} (L8) and \memitRhoLtwelve{} (L12),
3-seed means, both below the \deadThresh{} DEAD threshold --- versus ROME's
\romeCmpLeight{} (L8) / \romeCmpLtwelve{} (L12). Geometry does not predict
MEMIT collateral damage. The full DEAD layer profile is \rhoC{}
\memitRhoLeight{} / \memitRhoLten{} / \memitRhoLtwelve{} / \memitRhoLfourteen{}
(L8/L10/L12/L14; L8/L12 3-seed, L10/L14 single-seed).

\paragraph{Architecture arm.} The \emph{signed} law is Llama-specific. Signed
within-probe $\rho$, 3-seed: gemma-2-2b L13 \gemmaSigned{}\,$\pm$\,\gemmaSignedSD{},
Phi-3.5 L16 \phiSigned{}, Qwen-0.5B L12 \qwenHalfSigned{} (borderline),
Qwen-1.5B L14 \qwenFifteenSigned{}, Qwen-3B L18 \qwenThreeSigned{}. Eq.~\ref{eq:sc}
accounts for both breakdowns: near-null where $S\approx 0$ (distributed
storage, gemma/Phi), inversion where the edit sits in a net-improvement regime
(Qwen). The Qwen inversion is norm-growth-clean, scale-persistent, and
causally geometry-carried (\S\ref{sec:causal}).

\paragraph{The magnitude law transfers on four of five families.} The
unsigned magnitude law $|C|\rightarrow|\text{dmg}|$ is more portable
(Table~\ref{tab:magnitude}). \textbf{gemma is double-dead} --- geometry-blind
on both the signed (\gemmaSigned{}) and the magnitude
(\magGemma{}, perm-$p$ \magGemmaPermP{}, n.s.) law --- which strengthens the
anomaly rather than weakening the transfer story. Phi's canonical magnitude is
\magPhi{}, versus $\approx$\magPhiPeek{} in an unfiltered estimate that omitted
the known-fact filter.\footnote{The known-fact filter restricts to
probes the base model knows ($\text{pre\_p}>0.05$). Without it, Phi's magnitude
$\rho$ is $\approx$0.36. All headline numbers use both filters.}
