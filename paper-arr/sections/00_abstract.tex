% Abstract. Llama-family scoping stated up front (binding).
Locate-then-edit knowledge editors such as ROME insert a fact by a rank-one
update to one MLP layer, but that update also perturbs unrelated facts --
collateral damage. We ask \emph{when and why} a purely a-priori geometric
quantity, the cosine between an edit's key vector and an unrelated probe's
key, predicts that damage. For rank-one ROME updates \textbf{on the Llama
family}, we show the collateral perturbation factorizes in closed form into a
per-edit strength $S$, a per-probe norm, and the edit$\times$probe key-cosine
$C$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:sc}); the product \SxC{} is a zero-cost surrogate for
first-order (GradSim) gradient influence, computed with no backprop. On a
confound-clean within-probe metric the key-cosine predicts ROME damage across
four mid layers (Spearman $\rho$ up to \gWithinLtwelve{} at L12), undergoes a
depth/regime transition in which matrix-norm-growth overtakes it at L14, and
its \emph{sign} tracks the sign of the damage regime across model scale. The
law is \textbf{conditional on editor and architecture}: it vanishes for
Adam full-rank fine-tuning and for MEMIT's multi-layer spread, is near-null on
gemma and Phi, and \emph{inverts} on Qwen. A causal test closes the loop:
null-space projection (AlphaEdit) removes the geometry-predicted damage in
proportion to key-cosine at every layer, erases the Qwen inversion, and
collapses the coupling of a refusal-\emph{deletion} edit that was never in the
original design. We position the account as complementary to the concurrent
empirical predictor CLaRE-ty, contributing the mechanism, the
editor/architecture dissociation, and the causal test that it leaves open.
